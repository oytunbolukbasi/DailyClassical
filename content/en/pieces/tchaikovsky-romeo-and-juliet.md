### The big picture

- In 1869 Mily Balakirev, the leader of the group of Russian composers in St Petersburg, suggested *Romeo and Juliet* to the 29-year-old Tchaikovsky. He sent detailed advice on the plan and even on the keys, and Tchaikovsky dedicated the work to him.
- The first version was played in Moscow on 16 March 1870, conducted by Nikolai Rubinstein, and made little impression. Tchaikovsky revised it twice. The version played today dates from 1880 and was first heard in Tbilisi in 1886.
- It does not tell the play scene by scene. It picks three ideas: a solemn hymn for Friar Laurence, a violent theme for the feud between the two families, and a love theme for Romeo and Juliet, and sets them against each other in [[sonata form]].
- The love theme became one of the most famous melodies Tchaikovsky wrote, and film and television have borrowed it ever since.
- In one line: the friar's hymn, the street fight, the love song, the fight again, and a funeral march for the lovers.

### I. Romeo and Juliet

**Summary.** A slow introduction, then a fast and stormy main section, then a slow epilogue. The fast section is in [[sonata form]]: the feud is the first theme, the love song the second, and when both come back the feud wins. In the epilogue the love theme returns one last time, broken, over a funeral drumbeat.

**Main ideas**

- Friar Laurence: a slow, hymn-like [[chorale]], first in clarinets and bassoons.
- The feud: a fast, angry theme in B minor, with short stabbing chords and crashing cymbals like clashing swords.
- The love theme: a long, rising melody in D-flat major, first on English horn and violas together.
- The bedroom music: soft, rocking chords in [[muted]] strings, like a sigh.

**Things to notice**

1. The friar's hymn returns in the middle of the fight, loud in the brass. Listen for slow notes cutting through fast ones.
2. The love theme appears three times: tender in the exposition, at full strength in the recapitulation, and broken in the epilogue. Compare how it sounds each time.

**In this recording.** Semyon Bychkov recorded the overture with the Czech Philharmonic in Prague, on the first album of his "Tchaikovsky Project", a cycle of the symphonies, the piano concertos and other orchestral works. On the Spotify album it is track 5, after the *Pathétique* Symphony.

### Threads

- **Three ideas, one story.** Tchaikovsky reduced the play to three forces: the friar, the feud and the love. The drama is in how they meet.
- **Minor to major, but not a happy ending.** The overture begins in B minor and ends in B major. The major here sounds like peace after death, not victory.
- **Ten years of revision.** The overture took three versions and more than a decade to reach its final form. The love theme survived every revision.
- **Tchaikovsky and Shakespeare.** He returned to Shakespeare later, in *The Tempest* (1873) and *Hamlet* (1888).
