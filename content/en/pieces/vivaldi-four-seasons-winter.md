### The big picture

- *Winter* is the last of the four concertos known as *The Four Seasons*. Vivaldi published them in Amsterdam in 1725 as the first four of the twelve concertos of his Op. 8, *Il cimento dell'armonia e dell'inventione* ("The contest between harmony and invention"). He probably wrote them some years earlier.
- Like the other three, it comes with a sonnet, possibly by Vivaldi himself, whose lines are printed in the score above the music that illustrates them. Winter's sonnet has three scenes: freezing in a bitter wind, stamping your feet, teeth chattering; resting by the fire while the rain pours outside; walking carefully on the ice, slipping, falling and hearing all the winds at war. "This is winter," it ends, "but of a kind that brings joy."
- It is a concerto in the Baroque way: a refrain for the string band, the [[ritornello]], alternates with solo passages, and the solo violin plays most of the pictures. The harpsichord and bass instruments, the [[continuo]], keep everything together.
- The slow movement is one of Vivaldi's best-known tunes. Vivaldi reused the first movement himself, in the aria "Gelido in ogni vena" ("I feel my blood run cold in every vein"), which he put into his opera *Farnace* when it was staged again in 1731.
- In one line: shivering in the storm (I), warmth by the fire (II), slipping on the ice and the battle of the winds (III).

### I. Allegro non molto

**Summary.** "To tremble, frozen, in the icy snow." The movement opens with quiet repeated notes that pile up into harsh, clashing chords, the sound of shivering. The solo violin answers with wild runs, the bitter wind, and later with rapid repeated notes for the chattering teeth, while the band stamps its feet with loud, hammered chords.

**Main ideas**

- The shiver: soft, quick repeated notes in the strings, entering one part after another until the harmony grinds.
- The wind: fast, jagged runs and broken chords in the solo violin.
- The stamping: loud, heavy chords from the whole band, with the soloist rushing over them.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | Very quiet, rapid repeated notes; one instrument after another joins until the chords clash and shudder | "Frozen, trembling in the icy snow": the opening [[ritornello]] |
| ≈ 0:30 | The solo violin bursts in with fast, jagged runs | "The harsh breath of a horrid wind" |
| ≈ 0:45 | The shivering chords again | The ritornello returns |
| ≈ 1:00 | Loud, heavy chords hammered by the whole band; the soloist races above | "Running and stamping one's feet" |
| ≈ 1:40 | The solo violin rushes on alone in quick, swirling figures | More wind |
| ≈ 2:25 | The solo violin plays very fast repeated notes on two strings at once, rattling and nervous | "Teeth chattering in the bitter cold" |
| Last 20 seconds | The whole band, loud and shuddering, to the end | The closing ritornello |

**Things to notice**

1. In the first bars, listen to how the chord is built: each new part makes it harsher, until the whole band shivers on a dissonance.
2. The chattering teeth come near the end. Vivaldi writes rapid repeated notes on two strings, and the effect is closer to a shudder than a tune.

### II. Largo

**Summary.** Indoors, by the fire, "while the rain outside soaks everyone". The violins of the band pluck their strings softly, like raindrops on the window, the lower strings hold warm, quiet notes, and the solo violin sings one of Vivaldi's most famous melodies above them.

**Main ideas**

- The rain: the violins playing [[pizzicato]], gently and evenly.
- The song: a calm, singing melody in the solo violin, in E-flat major.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | Gentle plucked notes, soft and even; the solo violin begins a warm, calm melody | The rain outside, the fire inside. The violins of the band play [[pizzicato]] |
| ≈ 0:55 | The melody climbs higher and reaches its widest phrase | The middle of the song |
| Near the end | The melody comes down and closes softly; the plucking stops | The scene fades |

**Things to notice**

1. Listen to the two layers: the plucked raindrops never change their pace, while the melody above them stretches and lingers.

### III. Allegro

**Summary.** Out on the ice. The solo violin begins alone over a single held bass note, taking small, careful steps. Then it slips and falls, gets up, runs, and the ice cracks. A slower, gentler passage brings the warm south wind; then all the winds break loose together and the concerto, and the whole cycle, ends in a storm.

**Main ideas**

- The careful walk: a smooth, even line in the solo violin over one long bass note.
- The fall: sudden quick notes tumbling downwards.
- The winds at war: rushing scales and loud chords from the whole band.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | The solo violin alone over one long, held bass note, moving in small, smooth steps | "We walk on the ice, slowly, for fear of falling" |
| ≈ 0:30 | Sudden fast notes tumbling down | "We slip and crash to the ground" |
| ≈ 0:45 | The band joins in; the music speeds up | "We get up and run hard across the ice" |
| ≈ 1:05 | Quick, crackling figures from the whole band | "Until the ice cracks and breaks open" |
| ≈ 1:20 | Slower and calmer: a warm, gentle tune | The south wind, the Sirocco, blows through the doors |
| ≈ 1:55 | Fast, rushing scales and loud chords from everyone | "Sirocco, Boreas and all the winds at war" |
| Last 15 seconds | A loud, firm end in F minor | "This is winter, but it brings its own joys" |

**Things to notice**

1. At the very start, listen to the bass. It holds one note for a long time, like the frozen surface the walker is trying to cross.

**In this recording.** Simon Standage plays all four concertos with The English Concert on period instruments, and Trevor Pinnock directs from the harpsichord. *Winter* is tracks 10–12 of the Spotify album; *Spring* is tracks 1–3.

### Threads

- **The sonnet.** Every scene follows a line of the poem, in order: the shiver, the wind, the stamping, the chattering teeth, the fire and the rain, the walk on the ice, the fall, the cracking ice, the winds.
- **Cold and warm.** Two fast movements in F minor frame one slow movement in a warm major key. The contrast is the point: inside and outside.
- **The violin as painter.** The soloist plays the wind, the chattering teeth, the careful steps and the fall. The band paints the frozen air around them.
