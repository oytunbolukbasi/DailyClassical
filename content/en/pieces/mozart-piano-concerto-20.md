### The big picture

- Mozart wrote the concerto in early 1785 for his own concerts in Vienna and played it for the first time on 11 February 1785 in the concert hall of the Mehlgrube, a building on the Neuer Markt.
- His father Leopold, who had just arrived on a visit, wrote that the copyist was still at work on the parts when he got there, and that Wolfgang had no time to play the [[rondo]] through before the concert.
- It is the first of Mozart's two piano concertos in a minor key; the other is No. 24 in C minor. Some of his darkest music is in D minor: the Queen of the Night's vengeance aria in *The Magic Flute*, parts of *Don Giovanni*, the *Requiem*.
- The young Beethoven admired it and kept it in his repertoire. Mozart's own cadenzas are lost; Beethoven's are the ones most often played, and Brahms and Clara Schumann wrote cadenzas too.
- In one line: a storm that gathers in the dark (I), a tender song broken by a sudden squall (II), a fierce chase that breaks into D major at the very end (III).

### I. Allegro

**Summary.** The concerto opens not with a tune but with a murmur: low strings, uneasy off-beat rhythms, a growl in the basses. The storm breaks with the full orchestra, trumpets and drums. When the piano finally enters, it does not repeat the orchestra's music but brings a lonely theme of its own. The movement is a [[double exposition]] [[sonata form]] and ends quietly.

**Main ideas**

- The murmur: violins and violas restless on off-beat notes, with a short rising growl in the cellos and basses.
- The woodwind theme: a plaintive phrase passed between oboe, flute and bassoon.
- The piano's theme: a new, singing melody that only the piano plays, heard first when it enters.

2. The movement ends very softly, after all its storms.

She plays her own cadenzas. On the Spotify album the concerto is tracks 1–3, followed by Concerto No. 24.

### II. Romance

**Summary.** After the storm, a simple song in B-flat major. The piano sings it alone and the orchestra answers. It is a [[rondo]]: the song keeps returning, with two different episodes between. The second is a sudden storm in G minor. The trumpets and drums are silent throughout.

**Main ideas**

- The romance: a plain, tender tune, first played by the piano with no accompaniment.
- The storm: fast, rushing notes in the piano in G minor, with urgent chords in the orchestra.

### III. Rondo: Allegro assai

**Summary.** The piano shoots upwards from the bottom of the keyboard and the orchestra answers in fury. This rondo races through the minor, with a cheerful tune in F major as relief. After the cadenza the music finally turns to D major, and the concerto ends in brightness, not darkness.

**Main ideas**

- The rocket: a fast rising line in the piano, up the notes of the D minor chord.
- The dark second tune: restless and anxious, in the piano.
- The cheerful tune: a light, bright melody in F major, first in the orchestra; it returns at the end in D major.

### Threads

- **Darkness and light.** The concerto moves from the murmuring D minor of the opening to the D major of the last minute; the slow movement is a moment of calm between.
- **A dramatic voice.** The piano behaves like a character in an opera. It enters with its own tune and never plays the orchestra's opening alone.
- **Rockets.** The first movement's basses growl upwards and the finale's piano shoots upwards on the notes of the D minor chord; both are rising gestures from the same dark key.
