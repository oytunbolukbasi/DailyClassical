### The big picture

- Mozart finished it in Vienna on 9 March 1785, about four weeks after the dark D minor concerto, K. 466. He played it the next day, 10 March, at a concert of his own in Vienna's Burgtheater. Like the D minor concerto, it was written for his own concerts that season.
- It is a festive concerto in C major, with trumpets and drums in the orchestra, but much of it is quiet. The first movement opens with a march played softly, on tiptoe.
- Mozart's own cadenzas for it are lost, so every pianist has to supply them. Géza Anda plays his own in this recording.
- The slow movement became famous far beyond concert halls when the Swedish film *Elvira Madigan* (1967) used it, in this very recording by Anda. The concerto is still sometimes called the "Elvira Madigan" concerto.
- In one line: a quiet march that becomes a celebration (I), a long, dreaming song over muted strings (II), a bouncing comic-opera finale (III).

### I. Allegro maestoso

**Summary.** A grand first movement in [[sonata form]] with a [[double exposition]] that begins very quietly: a march on tiptoe in the strings. ### II. Andante

**Summary.** A long, dreaming melody over a soft, pulsing accompaniment. The violins play [[muted]], the middle strings pulse in groups of three, and the cellos and basses pluck. The piano takes up the song and wanders through distant keys before coming home.

**Main ideas**

- The melody: long and slow, with wide leaps, first in the muted first violins.
- The pulse: repeated notes in groups of three in the second violins and violas, with [[pizzicato]] in the cellos and basses.
- The wandering: in the middle, the piano drifts into minor keys, with sharp clashes in the harmony.

1. Listen under the melody. The pulse in threes hardly ever stops, like a heartbeat under the song.
2. In the middle the harmony rubs: notes that clash with each other and resolve late. They are part of the movement's ache.

**One word, many names.** Mozart called the movement simply "Andante". The name "Elvira Madigan" comes from the 1967 film, not from him.

### Threads

- **Quiet beginnings.** The first movement starts on tiptoe, the slow movement with muted strings. Mozart saves the brilliance for later.
- **Trumpets and drums.** They give the outer movements their festive ring, and they are silent in the Andante.
- **Who has the tune?** In the first movement the orchestra presents the march and the piano decorates it; in the Andante the piano takes over the song; in the finale the two share and trade it.
