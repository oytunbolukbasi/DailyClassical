### The big picture

- Rimsky-Korsakov wrote *Scheherazade* in the summer of 1888, at a country house south of St Petersburg. His score is dated between 4 June and 7 August.
- It is based on the frame story of *The Thousand and One Nights*, which he printed in the score. The Sultan Shahriar, convinced that all women are faithless, has each new wife killed after the wedding night. Scheherazade saves her life by telling him a story every night, for a thousand and one nights, until he gives up his vow.
- The two characters have their own themes. The Sultan is a heavy phrase played in [[unison]] by low brass and strings. Scheherazade is a solo violin, winding and ornate, over a harp. She returns between the tales, like a storyteller taking a breath.
- He gave the movements titles, then withdrew them. He wanted listeners to hear one Eastern fairy tale full of wonders, not four separate pictures. The titles are still used.
- As a young naval officer, Rimsky-Korsakov had spent almost three years at sea. The sea fills the first and last movements.
- In one line: the sea (I), a wanderer's tale (II), a love song (III), a festival and a shipwreck (IV).

### I. The Sea and Sinbad's Ship

**Summary.** The Sultan's stern theme, then Scheherazade's violin, then the sea. The Sultan's theme itself becomes the swell of the waves. The whole movement rolls like a ship under sail.

**Main ideas**

- The Sultan: a loud, heavy phrase that falls step by step, in the low brass and strings.
- Scheherazade: a long, curling violin solo over harp chords. Each character is tied to a theme, a [[leitmotif]].
- The sea: cellos rocking in broad waves under the Sultan's theme, now flowing.

**Summary.** The Sultan and Scheherazade confront each other, then the city erupts in a festival. Themes from earlier movements whirl past. The sea returns, the ship is wrecked on a rock, and in the quiet that follows, the violin has the last word.

**Things to notice**

1. Compare the Sultan's theme at the very start of the suite with its last appearance, under the final violin solo. The notes are the same; the mood has completely changed.

### Threads

- **Two voices.** The Sultan's heavy [[unison]] and Scheherazade's violin open the suite and close it. Everything in between is her story.
- **The sea.** It carries the first movement and wrecks the ship in the last.
- **Colour.** Rimsky-Korsakov was a master of orchestration and taught it for decades. Almost every instrument gets a solo moment.
