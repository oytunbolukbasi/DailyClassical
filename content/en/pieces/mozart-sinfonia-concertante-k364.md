### The big picture

- Mozart wrote it in Salzburg in 1779. He had just come home from a fruitless journey in search of a better post (1777–79), which took him to Mannheim and Paris. His mother, who travelled with him, died in Paris in July 1778.
- In Paris the [[sinfonia concertante]], a piece for several soloists and orchestra, was then highly fashionable. Mozart had tried something similar before, in a Concertone for two violins (1774), and he started another, for violin, viola and cello, that he left unfinished.
- The viola part is written in D major, a semitone lower than everyone else, and the instrument is tuned a semitone higher: a [[scordatura]]. The tighter strings make the viola brighter and help it carry over the orchestra. On modern instruments the retuning is less common.
- Mozart wrote out the cadenzas himself, so the soloists play his music there too; his autograph of the slow movement's cadenza is in the collection of the Veste Coburg.
- In one line: a grand opening from which the two soloists emerge together (I), a lament in C minor sung by both in turn (II), a quick, high-spirited chase (III).

### I. Allegro maestoso

**Summary.** A long, stately opening for the orchestra, with a slow build-up from soft to very loud. Then the violin and the viola appear together, almost unnoticed, on one high held note. From that moment the movement is a dialogue: one instrument says something, the other answers, a little lower or in a new key. It is laid out like a concerto movement, in [[sonata form]].

**Main ideas**

- The opening: firm, ceremonial chords for the whole orchestra, in a dotted rhythm.
- The build-up: a short figure repeated higher and higher over a held bass, growing louder.
- The dialogue: a melody played by the violin and answered straight away by the viola, or the other way round.

2. Follow one phrase through the dialogue. When the viola takes it over, the same notes sound warmer and darker.

**In this recording.** Father and son: Igor Oistrakh plays the violin, and his father David, best known as a violinist, plays the viola and also conducts. On the Spotify album the Sinfonia concertante is tracks 7–9, after the Violin Concertos Nos. 2 and 4 and before the Concertone.

### II. Andante

**Summary.** A slow lament in C minor, one of Mozart's saddest movements. The orchestra sets out the sighing melody, then the violin sings it, and the viola answers in its lower, darker register. The two soloists keep taking turns and then begin to overlap, until they share a long cadenza of their own.

**Main ideas**

- The lament: a slow, falling melody in C minor, first in the orchestra.
- The answer: the same melody passed from violin to viola, lower and darker.
- The consolation: a gentler passage in E-flat major.

### III. Presto

**Summary.** After the lament, a fast, bright finale. A bouncing tune comes round again and again, and between its returns the soloists race through quick passages, throwing short phrases to each other faster and faster.

**Main ideas**

- The main tune: quick and bouncing, first in the orchestra.
- The soloists' race: fast runs and leaps, passed from one instrument to the other.

### Threads

- **Two equal voices.** Neither instrument is the star. Each idea is offered by one and taken up by the other, often in turn, often a little lower.
- **The viola's bright tuning.** Mozart asked for the viola to be tuned up a semitone, to make its voice stronger next to the violin.
- **Echoes of the journey.** The sinfonia concertante was a Parisian fashion, and the long orchestral build-up of the first movement recalls the famous orchestra of Mannheim, both cities of Mozart's recent tour.
