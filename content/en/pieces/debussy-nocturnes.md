### The big picture

- The title does not mean the gentle piano nocturnes of Chopin. Debussy took it from the American painter James McNeill Whistler, who called his misty night views of the Thames "nocturnes". Debussy said the word should be heard in a "decorative" sense: the impressions and special effects of light that it suggests.
- The work took him seven years. It began around 1892 as "Three Scenes at Twilight"; in 1894 he planned it as a piece for the violinist Eugène Ysaÿe with orchestra, describing it as an experiment with a single colour, "what a study in grey would be in painting". In the end he wrote it for full orchestra and finished the score on 15 December 1899. He told his publisher that it was harder than writing a five-act opera.
- *Nuages* and *Fêtes* were first played in Paris on 9 December 1900 by the Lamoureux Orchestra under Camille Chevillard. *Sirènes* had to wait until 27 October 1901, because the women's choir it needs was not available. Debussy went on revising the score almost until he died.
- Each piece has a small story from Debussy himself. *Nuages* came to him on a stormy day, crossing the Pont de la Concorde in Paris: clouds driven by the wind and a boat's horn. *Fêtes* remembers a festival in the Bois de Boulogne, with a military band marching through the crowd. *Sirènes* is the sea, and the song of the sirens, sung by women's voices without words.
- In one line: grey clouds moving slowly across the sky (I), a bright, whirling festival with a parade passing through (II), the sea at night and the sirens' wordless song (III).

### I. Nuages

**Summary.** Debussy described "the unchanging aspect of the sky" and the slow, solemn movement of clouds, fading in grey tinged with white. A winding figure in the woodwinds drifts across the orchestra; a cor anglais answers with a short, sad call, like a boat's horn heard across the river. In the middle, flute and harp sing a simple tune together. Nothing ever gets loud.

**Main ideas**

- The clouds: a slow, rocking figure for clarinets and bassoons, moving in parallel chords.
- The horn call: a short, falling phrase on the cor anglais, each time a little different.
- The middle tune: a plain melody played by flute and harp in unison, built on the five notes of the [[pentatonic scale]].

### II. Fêtes

**Summary.** Debussy wrote of the "vibrating, dancing rhythm" of the air, with sudden flashes of light. The orchestra whirls in fast triplets. Then, from far away, a procession approaches: a quiet march rhythm on harps, drums and plucked basses, muted trumpets playing a fanfare, louder and louder until it fills the scene. The festival swallows the parade and slowly scatters.

**Main ideas**

- The whirl: fast, repeated three-note figures in the strings, with dancing tunes in the woodwinds on top.
- The procession: a march heard first in the distance, played by three muted trumpets over a soft, steady beat.

### III. Sirènes

**Summary.** The sea, with its countless rhythms, and the sirens' song among the moonlit waves. A small choir of women joins the orchestra, singing without words, on the open sound of a vowel. Their two-note swaying phrase rises and falls like the waves. In the middle the sea grows rougher; at the end the voices drift away, "laughing" as they pass, in Debussy's words.

**Main ideas**

- The waves: rocking figures in the strings and harps, never quite still.
- The sirens: women's voices in a [[vocalise]], a melody sung without words, often just two notes swinging back and forth.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |

1. The voices are used like instruments. Try to hear where the choir stops and the orchestra begins; often you cannot tell.

### Threads

- **Painting with sound.** The titles describe light and movement rather than events. Debussy borrowed his title from a painter who had borrowed it from music.
- **Never finished.** Debussy worked on the *Nocturnes* for seven years before they were published, and kept correcting and re-colouring the orchestration for years afterwards. A definitive edition appeared only in 1930, twelve years after his death.
- **Darkness to light to night.** Grey sky, a blaze of festival lights, and the moonlit sea: three moods of the same night.
