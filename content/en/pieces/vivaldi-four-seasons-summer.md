### The big picture

- *Summer* is the second of the four concertos known as *The Four Seasons*, published in Amsterdam in 1725 as the first four of the twelve concertos of Vivaldi's Op. 8, *Il cimento dell'armonia e dell'inventione* ("The contest between harmony and invention"). Like the others it was probably written some years earlier.
- Each concerto comes with a sonnet, possibly by Vivaldi himself, and he printed its lines in the score next to the music that paints them. Summer's sonnet is not about holidays. It is about the heat of the Italian countryside: people and flocks droop, the pines burn, birds sing, a cold north wind breaks in, and the shepherd weeps for fear of the storm. In the end the storm comes, with thunder and hail, and beats down the corn.
- It is a concerto in the Baroque way: a refrain for the string band, the [[ritornello]], alternates with passages for the solo violin, with the harpsichord and bass instruments, the [[continuo]], underneath. In *Summer* Vivaldi bends the plan: the first movement's opening refrain is the heat itself, and a second, violent refrain, the north wind, arrives halfway through and has the last word.
- It is the only one of the four concertos in which all three movements are in the same minor key, and the tension rises from start to finish.
- In one line: heat, birdsong and a rising wind (I), a sleepless shepherd and distant thunder (II), the hailstorm (III).

### I. Allegro non molto – Allegro

**Summary.** "Under the harsh season, set ablaze by the sun, man and flock languish." The movement opens with weary, broken phrases separated by silences: the heat. Then the soloist bursts out as the cuckoo, followed by the turtledove and the goldfinch. Gentle breezes stir, the north wind suddenly sweeps in, and the shepherd weeps in fear of the storm.

**Main ideas**

- The heat: short, sighing phrases in the strings, stopping and starting, as if too tired to go on. Vivaldi wrote "languor caused by the heat" over them.
- The birds: fast, bright solo passages: the cuckoo, then the turtledove, then the goldfinch.
- The north wind: a sudden, fierce rush of fast notes in the whole band.

1. Listen to the silences at the start. Vivaldi makes the heat audible by letting the music run out of breath.

### II. Adagio e piano – Presto e forte

**Summary.** The shepherd tries to rest, but "the fear of lightning and fierce thunder" and swarms of flies and gnats will not let him. The solo violin sings a slow, anxious line over the band's restless buzzing, and every few bars the whole band breaks in with a short, loud burst of thunder.

**Main ideas**

- The shepherd: a slow, sad melody in the solo violin.
- The flies: short, nagging repeated notes in the violins of the band, soft and insistent.
- The thunder: sudden, very fast and loud outbursts from everyone, then silence.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | A slow, mournful solo line; underneath, the violins buzz softly with small repeated notes | The tired shepherd and the flies |
| ≈ 0:20 | A sudden, loud, fast outburst from the whole band, then quiet again | Distant thunder |
| ≈ 0:45–1:40 | The pattern repeats: the lament, then thunder, a little more threatening each time | The storm drawing closer |
| Near the end | The thunder breaks in once more before the last quiet phrase | No rest for the shepherd |

**Things to notice**

1. The contrast is written into the title: *Adagio e piano*, slow and soft, against *Presto e forte*, fast and loud. Count how many times the thunder interrupts.

### III. Presto

**Summary.** "Alas, his fears were justified." The storm breaks: thunder, lightning and hail that cut down the corn. Vivaldi builds the whole movement from storm sounds, furious repeated notes, scales plunging downwards and broken chords racing up the solo violin, with hardly a melody in sight.

**Main ideas**

- The storm: fast, hammered repeated notes in the whole band, the sound of rain and hail.
- The lightning: scales that tear downwards, in the band and the solo violin.
- The soloist's flights: rapid broken chords and runs, wilder each time.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | The whole band explodes in furious repeated notes, then plunging scales | "The summer storm": the [[ritornello]] |
| ≈ 0:20 | The solo violin races up and down in broken chords across its strings | The first solo episode |
| ≈ 0:45 | The storm refrain again, in the band | Thunder and hail |
| ≈ 1:20 | For a moment something like a tune appears, then falls apart | The middle of the storm |
| ≈ 1:50 | The soloist's fastest, most violent runs | The last solo episode |
| Last 20 seconds | The whole band, hammering to a sudden end | The storm has done its damage |

**Things to notice**

1. Listen for the repeated notes. Fast, even repetition on one pitch, the [[tremolo]] of the strings, is Vivaldi's sound for heavy rain and hail.

**In this recording.** Simon Standage plays all four concertos with The English Concert on period instruments, and Trevor Pinnock directs from the harpsichord. *Summer* is tracks 4–6 of the Spotify album.

### Threads

- **The sonnet.** Every scene follows the poem in order: the heat, the cuckoo, the turtledove and goldfinch, the breezes, the north wind, the shepherd's tears, the flies and thunder, the hailstorm.
- **A storm that grows.** The north wind first appears in the middle of the first movement, thunder rumbles through the second, and the third is the storm itself.
- **One key.** All three movements stay in G minor, which gives *Summer* a darker, more anxious colour than the other three seasons.
