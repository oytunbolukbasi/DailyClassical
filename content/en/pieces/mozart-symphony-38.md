### The big picture

- Mozart finished the symphony in Vienna on 6 December 1786. He had begun the finale earlier that year, while he was writing *The Marriage of Figaro*.
- *Figaro* was a great success in Prague that winter, and a group of musicians and music lovers invited Mozart to the city. He conducted the first performance of the symphony there on 19 January 1787, at a concert the day after a performance of *Figaro*. That is how it got its name.
- It has only three movements: there is no [[minuet]]. That was very unusual for a symphony in Vienna at the time, and nobody knows for certain why Mozart left it out.
- The wind instruments have whole passages to themselves. Prague's wind players were famous, but Mozart had also been writing richer wind parts in his piano concertos.
- In one line: a long, grave introduction and a busy, brilliant Allegro (I), a gentle pastoral Andante with shadows (II), a quicksilver finale with the flute in the lead (III).

### I. Adagio – Allegro

**Summary.** The longest slow introduction Mozart ever wrote: grand at first, then darker, in the minor, before it comes to rest on a long, expectant chord. The Allegro begins quietly, over throbbing violins, and grows into a brilliant, crowded [[sonata form]] movement in which many short ideas are woven together.

**Main ideas**

- The introduction: a held D with a drumroll, solemn chords, and later a restless figure in the minor, loud and soft by turns.
- The first theme: a quiet, smooth tune in the lower strings over throbbing, off-beat notes in the first violins (a [[syncopation]]), answered by a short fanfare for the winds.
- The second theme: a gentle tune in A major, then repeated in A minor with the bassoon.

### II. Andante

**Summary.** A gentle, singing movement in G major, in a lilting six-beat rhythm. The trumpets and drums are silent. Its calm is broken from time to time by sudden heavy chords and dark wind entries, and in the middle the music wanders through minor keys.

**Main ideas**

- The first theme: a soft, singing tune in the strings that slides chromatically before it settles.
- The staccato figure: short, detached notes played by all the strings together, soon climbing in the first violin.
- The second theme: a hopeful, rising tune in the strings over a long, held bass note.

### III. Finale: Presto

**Summary.** A fast, light finale built on a tune of quick pick-up notes falling to a long one. Its opening recalls a hurried duet in *Figaro*, sung by two characters desperate to escape a locked room. Loud outbursts for the full orchestra alternate with quiet passages for the woodwinds alone, and the flute stands out.

**Main ideas**

- The main tune: three quick notes leading to a long one, then a falling, off-beat line; quiet, in the violins.
- The outbursts: the whole orchestra, with timpani, on the same figure.
- The woodwinds: flute, oboe and bassoon playing the tune by themselves.

**In this recording.** The Scottish Chamber Orchestra plays with natural horns and trumpets, the valveless brass of Mozart's time, and Mackerras takes the repeats throughout, which makes the first two movements long. He had recorded the symphonies once before with the Prague Chamber Orchestra.

### Threads

- **Prague.** The city loved *Figaro*, invited Mozart, and heard this symphony first. The finale's main tune recalls a duet from that opera.
- **Winds.** In all three movements the woodwinds have passages to themselves, without the strings.
- **Many voices.** Mozart weaves short ideas together in counterpoint in each movement, most of all in the first.
