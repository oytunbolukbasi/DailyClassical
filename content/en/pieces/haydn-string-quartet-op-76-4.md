### The big picture

- Haydn wrote the quartet in 1797, aged 65, after his second stay in London. It is the fourth of six quartets for the Hungarian Count Joseph Erdődy, published in 1799 as Op. 76; the third is the "Emperor".
- The name "Sunrise" is not Haydn's. It comes from the opening: the three lower instruments hold a quiet chord while the first violin's melody rises slowly above it.
- In the same year Haydn was at work on his oratorio *The Creation*, whose most famous moment is a sudden blaze of light. Some listeners hear the quartet's opening as a gentler sunrise of the same kind.
- In Germany it was once also called the "Tannhäuser" quartet, because its opening tune reminded listeners of a march in Wagner's opera, written half a century later.
- In one line: a slow sunrise and bright, busy music in turn (I), a searching, meditative Adagio (II), a sturdy minuet with a bagpipe-like trio (III), a cosy country tune that ends in a whirlwind (IV).

### I. Allegro con spirito

**Summary.** The movement is built on a contrast between two kinds of sound. In one, three instruments hold a soft chord and a single line rises slowly above it; in the other, all four play quick, lively figures together. The two take turns all through this [[sonata form]] movement.

**Main ideas**

- The sunrise: a long, rising melody in the first violin over a soft chord held by the other three. Later the long line moves down into the cello, winding downwards, with the violins and viola holding the chord above it.
- The busy music: quick runs and short, detached notes, tossed between the four players, loud and bright.

### II. Adagio

**Summary.** A slow movement in E-flat major that sounds more like a meditation than a song. The theme starts hesitantly, a few notes at a time with pauses between, and is then led through unexpected harmonies. The movement falls into two halves that begin the same way, the second one darker.

**Main ideas**

- The hesitant theme: three notes rocking around one pitch, then a held note with a pause, repeated a step higher.
- The ripples: quick, decorative figures in the first violin.
- The clashes: notes held over into the next chord, so the harmony grates for a moment before it resolves.

**Summary.** A brisk [[minuet]] built from a little two-note figure that rocks back and forth. At the end the music does not stop: one low note is held over into the [[trio]], where a folk-like tune floats over a drone, like a bagpipe.

**Main ideas**

### IV. Finale: Allegro ma non troppo – Più allegro – Più presto

**Summary.** The finale begins as an easy-going, cheerful tune, almost a country song. A middle section turns to the minor, with a few playful surprises. When the tune comes back it breaks off, and the quartet speeds up twice, ending in a whirl of fast notes.

**Main ideas**

- The tune: a comfortable, singing melody in the first violin, in two short halves, each repeated.
- The minor episode: the same kind of music in B-flat minor, more insistent.
- The whirlwind: faster and then faster still, with the tune's fragments spinning round.

### Threads

- **Held and moving.** The first movement sets long-held chords against busy music; the minuet's held note leads into a trio over a drone; the finale lets go of all stillness at the end.
- **Four voices.** The rising sunrise theme moves from the first violin to the cello and then up through the quartet. Listen for who has it.
- **Light.** The opening's slow rise, written while Haydn was working on *The Creation*, gave the quartet its name and colours how people have heard it ever since.
