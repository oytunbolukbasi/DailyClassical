### The big picture

- Prokofiev wrote the symphony in about a month in the summer of 1944, mostly at Ivanovo, a country retreat where the Union of Soviet Composers let musicians work away from the war. It was his first symphony since the Fourth of 1930, and he gave it the opus number 100.
- He called it "a hymn to free and happy Man, to his mighty powers, his pure and noble spirit", and also "a symphony on the greatness of the human soul".
- He conducted the premiere himself in Moscow on 13 January 1945. As he stood on the podium, artillery fired a salute outside for a Red Army victory, and he waited for the guns to fall silent before beginning. Soon afterwards he fell and suffered a concussion, and his health never fully recovered; it was his last appearance as a conductor.
- The symphony is built on a large scale, slow–fast–slow–fast, for a big orchestra with piano, harp and plenty of percussion. Its seriousness is real, but so is Prokofiev's old humour: the scherzo and the finale are full of wit, and the last pages turn wild.
- In one line: a broad, noble opening that ends in a roar (I), a mechanical, wry scherzo (II), a lyrical lament with a dark heart (III), a cheerful finale that ends in frenzy (IV).

### I. Andante

**Summary.** Unusually, the symphony begins slowly, and the first movement keeps a measured pace throughout. Its main theme is a calm, wide-spanning melody for flute and bassoon; a second, soaring theme follows. The music grows from these into a [[sonata form]] movement of great breadth, and it ends in a huge [[coda]] that roars with tam-tam and low piano.

**Main ideas**

- Theme 1: a calm, wide-ranging melody, first played by flute and bassoon together, in octaves.
- Theme 2: a soaring, wandering tune over trembling strings, its metre shifting.

1. The opening is played by two instruments at once, high and low. Listen for how that thin, tart sound grows into the full orchestra.

His tempos are broad, especially in the first and third movements. On the Spotify album the symphony is tracks 1–4, followed by Stravinsky's *The Rite of Spring*.

### II. Allegro marcato

**Summary.** A [[scherzo]] in Prokofiev's machine-like style. Under crisp, repeated notes in the violins, a clarinet plays a snaking, syncopated tune. In the middle the music turns into a jaunty dance in three, with snare drum and tambourine. When the opening returns it is heavier and more menacing, and it rushes to a sudden end.

**Main ideas**

- The motor: short, crisp repeated notes in the violins.
- The clarinet tune: a snaking, off-beat melody with a music-hall swagger.
- The trio: a jaunty tune in triple time.

### III. Adagio

**Summary.** The emotional centre of the symphony. A lyrical, slightly melancholy melody unfolds over a gently rocking accompaniment in triplets. Then the music darkens into something like a funeral march and climbs to a harsh, dissonant climax. The opening melody returns, and the movement ends quietly.

**Main ideas**

- The accompaniment: steady triplets, rocking gently underneath.
- The melody: long and singing, first heard in the woodwind with the clarinet prominent, later in the strings.
- The middle section: a slow tread like a funeral march.

2. After the climax, notice how the opening melody sounds different: the same notes, after a storm.

### IV. Allegro giocoso

**Summary.** "Fast and playful." After a quiet introduction in which the cellos recall the symphony's opening theme, the clarinet launches a cheerful main tune. It returns between contrasting episodes: a balletic idea on the flute, a broad hymn in the strings. Near the end the music speeds into a frenzy, and the last bars are given to a few solo strings ticking away, interrupted by trumpets and percussion, before a final unison B-flat.

**Main ideas**

- The introduction: the opening theme of the first movement, slow and hushed, in the cellos.
- The main tune: a cheerful, bouncing clarinet melody.
- The episodes: a light, dancing flute tune; a solemn hymn for the strings.

1. Listen for the first movement's theme in the slow opening. The finale begins by looking back.
2. In the last minute the full orchestra drops out and only a handful of string players are left, ticking like a clock. Many listeners hear the ending as more ironic than triumphant.

### Threads

- **The opening theme.** The calm melody of the first bars returns at the start of the finale, linking the beginning and the end.
- **Slow and fast.** Two slow movements and two fast ones alternate. The slow ones carry the symphony's weight; the fast ones keep Prokofiev's humour alive.
- **War and peace.** Written in 1944 and first heard as the guns of victory sounded outside, the symphony is both serious and confident, with shadows in its slow movements and wildness at its close.
