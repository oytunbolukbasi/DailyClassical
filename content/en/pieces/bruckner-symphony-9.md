### The big picture

- Bruckner began the symphony in August 1887 and worked on it, with long interruptions for revising his earlier symphonies, until his death in Vienna on 11 October 1896, aged 72. The first three movements were finished by November 1894; the finale was left as a mass of sketches.
- According to his early biographers, he said he was dedicating his last work “to the beloved God”, and hoped God would give him time to complete it.
- It is usually played, as here, in three movements: a vast first movement, a fierce [[scherzo]], and a slow Adagio that ends in peace. Bruckner called the Adagio's chorale his "farewell to life".
- Like Beethoven's Ninth, it is in D minor and puts the scherzo second. The orchestra has eight horns, four of which switch to [[Wagner tuba]]s in the Adagio.
- The first performance, in Vienna in 1903 under Ferdinand Löwe, used Löwe's heavily altered version. Bruckner's own score was not heard until 1932, in Munich.
- In one line: a dark, cathedral-sized struggle (I), a demonic dance with a ghostly trio (II), a long farewell that finds peace (III).

### I. Feierlich, misterioso

**Summary.** "Solemn, mysterious." Out of a trembling D, the horns intone a dark motif, and the music grows slowly to a huge theme played by the whole orchestra in octaves. Then come two more groups of themes, one yearning and one mysterious. The rest of the movement develops and restates them together, and the end is not a victory but a blazing, dissonant clash.

**Main ideas**

- The opening: a [[tremolo]] on D in the strings, with a slow, dark horn motif.
- The octave theme: a huge, falling theme for the whole orchestra in [[unison]], with a long-held note and a plunge of an octave.
- The second group: a broad, yearning melody in the violins, slower.
- The third group: quiet woodwind phrases over shimmering strings, then a mysterious, rocking theme in unison.

**Things to notice**

1. Listen to the silences. Bruckner often stops the whole orchestra dead, then starts again in a new colour, like an organist changing stops.
2. In the last bars the music does not resolve into warmth: it ends on bare fifths with a clashing note on top.

### II. Scherzo: Bewegt, lebhaft – Trio: Schnell

**Summary.** A menacing, stamping [[scherzo]]. It starts with a bar of silence, then strange, sour woodwind chords over plucked strings, before the whole orchestra hammers out a brutal rhythm. In the middle, the [[trio]] is fast, light and eerie, in the distant key of F-sharp major. Then the scherzo returns.

**Main ideas**

- The opening chord: a sour, unresolved woodwind chord, repeated, over [[pizzicato]] strings.
- The hammering: the full orchestra pounding the same chord in a heavy, stamping rhythm.
- The trio: quick, flickering string figures, light and ghostly, with a slower, singing middle.

1. Listen to the very first chord. It belongs to no single key, and Bruckner repeats it again and again without explaining it. Many listeners hear this scherzo as the most modern music Bruckner wrote.

### III. Adagio: Langsam, feierlich

**Summary.** "Slow, solemn." The violins open alone with an anguished upward leap. The movement unfolds in two great paragraphs, the first searching and painful, the second consoling, and they return and grow. At the height of the last climax the music breaks on a shattering discord. After a silence, the movement ends in calm.

**Main ideas**

- The opening theme: violins alone, leaping upward and then sinking through strange harmonies.
- The trumpet fanfare: a call repeated again and again over the whole orchestra.
- The "farewell to life": a slow, sinking [[chorale]] for horns and Wagner tubas.
- The consoling theme: a broad, warm melody in the strings.

### Threads

- **A symphony without an end.** Bruckner meant to write a finale, but died working on it. Played in three movements, the symphony ends with the slow Adagio, and many hear that as fitting.
- **Faith and farewell.** The dedication “to the beloved God”, the chorale Bruckner called his "farewell to life", the quiet end: this is the most personal of his symphonies.
- **Bold harmony.** The sour chord that opens the scherzo, the discord at the Adagio's climax: Bruckner, the conservative organist, wrote some of the most daring sounds of the 1890s.
