### The big picture

- Mozart probably wrote it in the early 1780s, most likely in Vienna; scholars long dated it to 1783, though the latest research suggests it may be a little earlier. It was published in Vienna in August 1784, together with the Sonatas K. 330 and K. 332.
- It breaks the usual pattern. None of its three movements is in [[sonata form]]: it opens with a theme and variations, continues with a [[minuet]], and ends with a [[rondo]]. All three stay in A, major or minor.
- The finale imitates the bands of the Janissaries, the Ottoman sultan's soldiers, with their drums, cymbals and shrill pipes. "Turkish" music was the height of fashion in Vienna; Mozart's opera *The Abduction from the Seraglio*, a hit in 1782, has two Janissary choruses.
- For two centuries only the last page of Mozart's manuscript was known. In 2014 a librarian in Budapest, Balázs Mikusi, found four more pages, and the pianist Zoltán Kocsis played the newly found text there that September.
- In one line: a tune in six disguises (I), a graceful dance (II), a march with an imaginary drum (III).

### I. Andante grazioso

**Summary.** A gentle, swaying tune followed by six [[variation]]s. Each variation keeps the tune's shape and length but changes its clothes: new rhythms, a turn to the minor, a slow, ornate version, and a fast, brilliant ending.

**Main ideas**

- The theme: a calm, rocking tune in 6/8, in the lilting rhythm of a [[siciliano]]. Two short halves, each played twice.
- The variations: six new versions of the same tune, each a little world of its own.

**Things to notice**

1. Try to hum the theme through each variation. Its shape is always there, even when it is buried in fast notes.
2. Variation 6 changes the metre: the rocking six becomes a brisk four, the clearest sign that the end is near.

### II. Menuetto

**Summary.** A stately [[minuet]] with wide-spaced chords and sudden loud and soft contrasts. The [[trio]] in the middle is gentler and moves to D major, with the hands crossing over each other.

**Main ideas**

- The minuet: firm chords and octaves, graceful but grand.
- The trio: a softer, singing tune in D major; the left hand reaches over the right.

**Things to notice**

1. Watch for the hand-crossing in the trio, if you are watching a pianist: the left hand jumps over the right to play the higher notes.

### III. Alla turca. Allegretto

**Summary.** The famous "Turkish" finale. A quick, nimble tune in A minor alternates with a loud, stamping march in A major, where the left hand's rolled chords imitate the crash of cymbals and drums. It is a [[rondo]]: the march keeps coming back.

**Main ideas**

- The minor tune: quick notes turning around a single note, light and nimble.
- The march: loud, in octaves, over rolled chords in the left hand, the "Janissary" sound.
- The coda: the march's rhythm hammered out, with the rolled chords at full force.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | A quick tune in the minor, circling around one note | The opening tune, in A minor |
| ≈ 0:45 | Loud and bright: a march in octaves, with rolled, crashing chords in the left hand | The refrain, in A major. Cymbals and drums, imagined on the piano |
| ≈ 1:00 | Running fast notes over broken chords, in a darker key | An episode in F-sharp minor |
| ≈ 1:45 | The loud march again | The refrain returns |
| ≈ 2:00 | The nimble minor tune once more | The opening section returns |
| ≈ 2:45 | The march, for the third time | The refrain |
| ≈ 3:02 | Hammered rhythm and crashing chords, then loud, final octaves | The [[coda]] |

**Things to notice**

1. Listen to the left hand in the march. The rolled chords, struck fast from bottom to top, are Mozart's way of making a piano sound like a Janissary band.

**Mozart's own word.** The early sources mark this movement *Allegrino*, "a little lively", an unusual word; the heading "Alla turca" appears to have been added when the sonata was printed. Pianos with a "Janissary stop", a pedal that worked a drum, cymbals and a triangle, were built only later, in the 19th century. Mozart's drum is in the notes themselves.

### Threads

- **One key.** All three movements stay in A major or A minor. Variety comes from rhythm and colour, not from changing key.
- **Disguises.** The first movement dresses one tune six ways; the finale turns the piano into a military band.
- **Two hands.** In the fourth variation and in the trio the left hand crosses over the right; in the finale it becomes the drummer.
