### The big picture

- Mozart finished the quintet in Vienna on 29 September 1789, for his friend Anton Stadler, the clarinettist for whom he later wrote the Clarinet Concerto. In a letter of April 1790 Mozart called it "Stadler's quintet".
- It was first played on 22 December 1789 at a charity concert in Vienna for the widows and orphans of musicians, with Stadler as soloist. It was heard between the two halves of a cantata by another composer.
- Mozart wrote it for the [[basset clarinet]], Stadler's instrument with extra low notes. Today it is usually played on an ordinary clarinet in A.
- This is not a concerto. The clarinet leads more often than the others, but the five players share the music, and the strings often have the tune.
- In one line: a calm, singing Allegro (I), a slow song for clarinet over muted strings (II), a minuet with two contrasting trios (III), a set of variations on a cheerful tune (IV).

### I. Allegro

**Summary.** The strings begin alone with a calm, hymn-like theme, and the clarinet answers with a quick, rising and falling sweep of notes. That contrast, between the strings' smooth chords and the clarinet's brilliant runs, carries the whole movement. It is in [[sonata form]].

**Main ideas**

- The strings' theme: soft, smooth chords, moving together like a hymn.
- The clarinet's answer: a fast [[arpeggio]] that climbs up and falls back.
- The second theme: a gentle, slightly wistful tune, first in the violin over a plucked cello, then in the clarinet.

### II. Larghetto

**Summary.** A long, slow song for the clarinet over [[muted]] strings, in D major. In the middle the first violin answers with rising runs, and the two seem to talk. Near the return of the song, the clarinet takes up the violin's runs for itself.

**Main ideas**

- The song: a slow, calm melody in the clarinet, with soft chords in the muted strings.
- The dialogue: quick upward scales in the first violin, answered by short clarinet phrases.

### III. Menuetto – Trio I – Trio II

**Summary.** An elegant [[minuet]] with, unusually, two [[trio]]s. The first is for the strings alone, in A minor; the clarinet stays silent. The second brings the clarinet back with a rustic, country-dance tune. The minuet returns after each trio.

**Main ideas**

- The minuet: a firm, graceful dance for all five.
- Trio I: a shadowy tune for the strings alone, with a little grace note that gives it a sighing, catching sound.
- Trio II: a lilting, rustic dance tune in the clarinet.

### IV. Allegretto con variazioni

**Summary.** A set of [[variation]]s on a cheerful, march-like tune. In each variation the tune keeps its shape but changes its dress: new counter-melodies, a minor-key lament for the viola, fast runs, a slow Adagio. A short, quicker passage ends the work.

**Main ideas**

- The theme: a bright, simple tune in the strings, in two short halves, each repeated; the clarinet joins only for a few bars.

### Threads

- **A friend's voice.** The quintet was written for Stadler's playing: long, singing lines and quick runs across the whole range of his instrument.
- **Five equals.** The strings often lead, and in the first trio the clarinet does not play at all.
- **Smooth and brilliant.** The first movement's contrast, smooth string chords against the clarinet's sparkling runs, comes back in the Larghetto's dialogue and in the finale's variations.
