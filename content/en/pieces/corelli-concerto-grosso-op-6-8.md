### The big picture

- Arcangelo Corelli was the most famous violinist in Rome, and his music was copied and admired all over Europe. His twelve concerti grossi, Op. 6, were probably written in the 1680s; he prepared them for print late in life, but they appeared in Amsterdam in 1714, a year after his death, dedicated to the Elector Palatine, Johann Wilhelm.
- A [[concerto grosso]] sets a small group of soloists against the full string band. Here the soloists, the concertino, are two violins and a cello; the band, the ripieno, can be as large as the occasion allows. Sometimes they play together, sometimes the small group answers the large one, like an echo.
- The score of No. 8 is headed *Fatto per la notte di Natale*, "made for the night of Christmas". Corelli lived and worked for years in the palace of his patron Cardinal Pietro Ottoboni. He is recorded as playing a Christmas concerto for the cardinal in 1690, and the work is traditionally linked with Christmas Eve at Ottoboni's palace.
- The last movement, the [[pastorale]], is marked *ad libitum*: it could be left out when the concerto was not played at Christmas. It imitates the *pifferari*, the bagpipe and reed-pipe players whose music belonged to Christmas in Italy: a sweet tune in pairs over a long-held bass. Handel took up the same idea in the *Pifa* of *Messiah*, and his own Op. 6 concerti grossi (1739) were modelled on Corelli's.
- In one line: a solemn opening (I), a quick chase (II), a tender song (III), two quick dances (IV–V), a shepherds' pastorale (VI).

### I. Vivace – Grave

**Summary.** A few bars of quick, firm chords, then a long, slow Grave. In the Grave the strings hold their notes and lean into clashes that slowly resolve, one after another. Corelli marked it *Arcate, sostenuto e come stà*: bowed, sustained and played as written, without added ornaments.

**Main ideas**

- The Vivace: a short burst of chords for everyone.
- The Grave: long, held notes that rub against each other and then resolve, in a slow chain.

**Listening stops**

1. In the Grave, listen for the moment of tension when one note is held over against the next chord, and the small release when it moves down.

### II. Allegro

**Summary.** A quick movement in which the two solo violins chase each other, one copying the other a moment later, over a busy bass. The full band joins in to drive the music on.

**Main ideas**

- The chase: a quick figure in one violin, taken up at once by the other.
- The bass: running, steady notes underneath.

**Listening stops**

**Things to notice**

1. Follow the two violins. Their lines cross and overlap so closely that it is hard to tell which one is leading.

### III. Adagio – Allegro – Adagio

**Summary.** The heart of the concerto, in a warm E-flat major. The two violins sing a slow, tender duet over a quietly pulsing bass. A quicker middle section brings more movement and some unrest, and then the slow duet returns.

**Main ideas**

- The duet: two violins singing close together, often in thirds, over soft, even steps in the bass.
- The middle section: quicker, with busy figures in the violins.

### V. Allegro

**Summary.** A lively finale in quick two-time, in which the solo violins run in fast figures and the band answers with strong, short phrases. It closes in G minor, and the Pastorale that follows will change everything to major.

**Main ideas**

- The running figures: quick notes in the solo violins, tossed back and forth.
- The band's answers: firm, short chords.

### VI. Pastorale: Largo

**Summary.** The Christmas music proper. The key turns to G major and the rhythm to a gentle rocking in twelve slow beats to the bar. Over a long-held bass, like the drone of a bagpipe, the violins play a sweet tune in pairs, as the *pifferari* did on their pipes. At the end the music slows and fades away.

**Main ideas**

- The pastorale tune: a gently rocking melody in two violins, moving together in thirds.
- The drone: long, held notes in the bass, like a bagpipe.

**Listening stops**

**Things to notice**

1. Listen to the lowest notes. For long stretches they do not move at all, just as a bagpipe's drone does not.
2. Compare the start of this movement with the end of the last one. The key has turned from minor to major.

### Threads

- **Small group, large band.** In every movement the concertino of two violins and a cello sets off against the full ripieno, sometimes leading, sometimes echoing.
- **Two violins as one voice.** Again and again the two solo violins move together a third apart, in the tender Adagio and in the Pastorale.
- **From church to Christmas.** The concerto follows the pattern of a church concerto, slow and fast movements in turn, and the optional Pastorale turns it into music for one night of the year.
