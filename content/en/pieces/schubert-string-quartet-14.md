### The big picture

- Schubert wrote it in March 1824, after a year of serious illness, poverty and failures. In a letter of that time he described himself as a man whose health would never return and whose brightest hopes had come to nothing.
- The name comes from the second movement: [[variation]]s on the piano part of his 1817 [[lied]] "Der Tod und das Mädchen". In Matthias Claudius's poem a girl begs Death to pass her by, and Death answers gently that he comes as a friend.
- It was first played in January 1826 at a private house in Vienna and published only in 1831, three years after Schubert's death. The leading violinist Ignaz Schuppanzigh, the story goes, told him to stick to his songs.
- A triplet rhythm runs through all four movements, and the finale is a [[tarantella]], a whirling dance in 6/8.
- In one line: a struggle (I), Death's song and five variations (II), a fierce dance (III), a dance of death (IV).

### I. Allegro

**Summary.** A movement in [[sonata form]] that lurches between violence and tenderness. A short, hammering triplet figure from the first bar drives almost everything, even under the gentle second theme.

**Main ideas**

- The opening: a loud [[unison]] D and a hammering triplet figure, then a sudden, hushed [[chorale]].
- Theme 1: a quiet line over a restless ripple of triplets in the lower instruments.
- Theme 2: a gentle, lilting tune for the two violins in thirds, in F major.

### IV. Presto

**Summary.** A relentless [[tarantella]] in [[rondo]] form: the main tune returns between other sections, and the speed only increases. A last, even faster rush ends in D minor.

**Main ideas**

- The main tune: a galloping, limping dance in 6/8, first played by all four together.
- The second theme: a broad, chorale-like melody in long notes; later the first violin runs in triplets around it.

### Threads

- **The triplet.** A rhythm of three quick notes appears in every movement, from the first bar to the galloping finale.
- **Loud against soft.** Sudden switches from *fortissimo* to *pianissimo* run through the whole quartet.
- **Dances with death.** A death march in the variations, a fierce scherzo and a tarantella: Death's music keeps turning into dance.
