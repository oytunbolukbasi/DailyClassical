### The big picture

- In 1897 the premiere of Rachmaninoff's First Symphony was a disaster, and for three years he could hardly compose. The Second Piano Concerto (1901) restored his confidence, but a new symphony was the real test.
- In 1906 he moved with his family to Dresden, away from the busy musical life and the political unrest of Moscow, to have time to compose. He wrote the Second Symphony there between October 1906 and April 1907 and dedicated it to his old teacher Sergei Taneyev.
- He conducted the first performance himself in St Petersburg in February 1908. It was a success, and later that year it won him a Glinka Award.
- For decades the symphony was usually played with large cuts, sometimes down to about 35 minutes. Since around 1970 the complete text has become the norm. This recording plays it complete, without the first-movement repeat.
- A short, dark [[motto]] from the opening returns in every movement and ties the four together.
- In one line: shadows that slowly lift (I), a galloping [[scherzo]] (II), a long love song (III), a festival (IV).

### I. Largo – Allegro moderato

**Summary.** A slow, dark introduction grows from a few notes in the cellos and basses. When the faster main section begins, its first theme turns out to be made from the same notes. The movement is in [[sonata form]], with a stormy development and a coda that ends in E minor with two loud outbursts.

**Main ideas**

- The motto: a short, sighing phrase rising and falling in the low strings, heard at the very start.
- Theme 1: a restless, surging violin melody, built from the motto.
- Theme 2: a gentle, swaying melody in the major, over soft [[pizzicato]] in the lower strings.

### II. Allegro molto

**Summary.** A fast, energetic movement in the role of a [[scherzo]], though in two beats rather than three. A galloping horn tune alternates with a broad, singing melody; in the middle comes a busy section where the instruments chase one another. At the end the brass quietly play a chant-like phrase, and the music vanishes.

**Main ideas**

- The galloping tune: a bold horn melody over a rushing [[ostinato]] in the strings. It is related to the medieval chant [[Dies irae]].
- The song: a broad, warm melody for the violins.
- The chase: quick, overlapping entries in the middle section, like a [[fugato]].

1. Count the beats in the galloping tune: two in a bar, not the three of most scherzos.
2. Remember the quiet brass phrase at the end. It comes back, loud, in the last minute of the symphony.

### III. Adagio

**Summary.** One of the best-loved slow movements in the repertoire. After a short, yearning phrase in the violins, a solo clarinet sings a long, winding melody that seems never to end. The motto returns in the middle and leads to a climax; then the melodies return, interwoven.

**Main ideas**

- The violins' phrase: a short, rising, yearning line that opens the movement.
- The clarinet song: a long melody that circles around single notes and keeps unfolding.
- The motto from the first movement, now gentler.

**Things to notice**

1. Follow the clarinet solo from start to finish. It lasts around two minutes without a real pause.
2. When the clarinet's melody returns in the violins, listen for the opening phrase woven around it in other instruments.

### IV. Allegro vivace

**Summary.** A bright, festive finale in E major. A galloping first theme, a march, and then one of Rachmaninoff's broadest melodies. Themes from the earlier movements reappear, and the symphony ends in a blaze of E major.

**Main ideas**

- Theme 1: a quick, leaping, festive tune in the full orchestra.
- The march: a short, tripping episode in the woodwinds.
- Theme 2: a broad, sweeping melody in the strings.
- The falling scale: long descending scales, like peals of bells, in the development.

2. The brass chant at the very end is the same phrase that ended the scherzo quietly. Here it is loud and triumphant.

### Threads

- **The motto.** The low-string phrase of the opening returns in all four movements, darker or gentler, and links them into one story.
- **From E minor to E major.** The first movement ends in the minor. The finale turns the same key to the major and ends in celebration.
- **Long melodies.** The clarinet's song in the Adagio and the broad string tune of the finale keep unfolding, the hallmark of Rachmaninoff's style.
- **A comeback.** After the failure of the First Symphony, this symphony's success confirmed Rachmaninoff as a composer of large orchestral works.
