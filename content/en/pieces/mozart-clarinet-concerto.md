### The big picture

- Mozart finished the concerto in October 1791, about two months before his death. It is his last concerto and one of the last works he completed.
- He wrote it for his friend Anton Stadler, a Viennese clarinettist, who played a special instrument with extra low notes: the [[basset clarinet]]. Mozart's own score is lost. When the concerto was first printed, around 1801, the solo part was rewritten for the ordinary clarinet, and many of the lowest passages were moved up.
- The first movement grew out of an unfinished concerto for basset horn, a larger relative of the clarinet, that Mozart had abandoned after about 200 bars.
- The orchestra is light: two flutes, two bassoons, two horns and strings. There are no oboes, trumpets or drums.
- In one line: a warm, spacious Allegro (I), a slow song of great simplicity (II), a playful rondo with shadows (III).

### I. Allegro

**Summary.** A broad, sunny first movement in [[sonata form]] with a [[double exposition]]: the orchestra plays the main tunes first, then the clarinet enters and makes them its own. The clarinet sings, but it also leaps from its bright top notes to its dark, woody bottom notes, as if two characters were talking.

**Main ideas**

- Theme 1: a warm, gently rocking tune in the strings, soon taken up by the whole orchestra.
- The clarinet's range: sudden leaps between high and very low notes, and fast runs that sweep across the whole instrument.
- Theme 2: a quieter, more wistful tune that the clarinet introduces in the solo exposition.

**Things to notice**

1. Listen for the clarinet's low notes. Ordinary clarinets cannot play the lowest of them; Thea King plays a basset clarinet, the instrument Mozart wrote for.
2. When the clarinet leaps from high to low, it can sound like a question and an answer from two different voices.

### II. Adagio

**Summary.** A slow song in three parts. The clarinet sings a simple melody, the orchestra repeats it, and a freer middle section lets the clarinet roam across its range before the melody returns.

**Main ideas**

- The song: a slow, simple melody in the clarinet over soft strings.
- The middle section: longer, more ornamented lines that dip into the clarinet's low register.

### III. Rondo: Allegro

**Summary.** A lively finale in a skipping six-beat rhythm. It is a [[rondo]]: the clarinet's bouncing refrain keeps coming back, with episodes in between, and one of them turns unexpectedly dark.

**Main ideas**

- The refrain: a cheerful, bouncing tune, played first by the clarinet and then by the orchestra.
- The episodes: quick runs and leaps for the clarinet, and one passage in a minor key that clouds the mood.

### Threads

- **A voice with two registers.** In every movement the clarinet moves between its bright upper notes and its dark lower ones.
- **Singing.** The Adagio is a song without words, and the outer movements keep returning to long, singing lines.
- **Shadows in the sun.** Each movement is mainly bright, but each has a moment where the music darkens.
