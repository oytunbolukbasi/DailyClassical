### The big picture

- Brahms wrote the symphony in the summer of 1883 in Wiesbaden, on the Rhine, six years after his Second. He was 50. The Vienna Philharmonic under Hans Richter gave the first performance in Vienna on 2 December 1883.
- It opens with three rising notes, F–A-flat–F, that return all through the work. They are usually read as Brahms's own motto, *frei aber froh*, "free but happy", an answer to his friend Joseph Joachim's motto *frei aber einsam*, "free but lonely". Brahms himself never explained them.
- Major and minor fight from the first bar: the motto uses the A-flat of F minor, the tune that follows the A-natural of F major. The finale starts firmly in F minor and only reaches F major in its last minutes.
- It is the shortest of Brahms's four symphonies, and all four movements end quietly, which was very unusual. Richter called it Brahms's "Eroica"; the critic Eduard Hanslick thought it "artistically the most nearly perfect" of the symphonies so far.
- In one line: a heroic, restless start that sinks to rest (I), a calm hymn for the woodwinds (II), a sad, swaying song (III), a stormy finale that finds peace (IV).

### I. Allegro con brio

**Summary.** Two great chords from the winds lift the three-note [[motto]], and the violins plunge down from the top in a sweeping, passionate theme. The movement is a [[sonata form]] full of rhythms that pull against the beat, and of sudden shadows when major turns to minor. After all its energy, it ends softly.

**Main ideas**

- The motto: three notes, F, A-flat, F, rising in big steps; it sits under or over almost everything.
- Theme 1, in the violins: two wide downward sweeps, loud and heroic, with the motto in the bass.
- Theme 2, on the clarinet: a gentle, swaying tune in a slow-feeling triple rhythm, over plucked low strings.

**In this recording.** Klemperer recorded the symphony with the Philharmonia in Kingsway Hall, London, in March 1957. He takes the repeat of the exposition, which many conductors leave out.

### II. Andante

**Summary.** A slow, quiet movement led by the woodwinds. Clarinets and bassoons play a simple, hymn-like theme, and the low strings answer each phrase like an echo. A second, more solemn idea appears for a moment; the finale will bring it back.

**Main ideas**

- Theme 1: a plain, folk-like melody on the clarinet, with bassoons, each phrase echoed by the low strings.
- Theme 2: a stern, slow tune played in [[unison]] by clarinet and bassoon, like a [[chorale]].

- The song: a long, sad melody in C minor, first in the cellos, over a quietly flowing accompaniment.

### IV. Allegro – Un poco sostenuto

**Summary.** The finale begins in a whisper, in F minor, and soon turns into a storm. The solemn chorale from the second movement breaks in twice. The coda slows down, settles into F major, and in the last bars the main theme of the first movement returns, now soft and shimmering.

**Main ideas**

- Theme 1: a hushed, scurrying theme in the strings and bassoons, in unison.
- The chorale: the solemn tune from the second movement, now in the trombones.
- Theme 2: a heroic, striding tune for cellos and horn, in triplets.

### Threads

- **The motto.** F–A-flat–F opens the symphony and runs through the first movement; in the last bars of the finale it lifts the music once more before the first movement's theme returns.
- **Major and minor.** A-flat against A, F minor against F major. The struggle that begins in the first bar is only settled in the finale's coda.
- **Quiet endings.** Each movement ends softly. The finale, with all its storm, does the same: Brahms closes the symphony with calm, not triumph.
- **Themes that return.** The second movement's chorale comes back in the finale, and the first movement's theme closes the whole work. Brahms built this symphony as one journey.
