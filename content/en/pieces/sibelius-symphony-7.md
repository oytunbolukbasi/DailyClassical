### The big picture

- Sibelius first mentioned the Seventh in his diary in December 1917, while still revising the Fifth. In 1918 he imagined it in three movements, full of "joy of life and vitality"; in the early 1920s he planned four. Most of the music came from the planned slow movement, and around 1923 he decided to fuse everything into a single movement.
- He finished the score on 2 March 1924, often working at night, and conducted the premiere himself in Stockholm on 24 March 1924, with the orchestra of the Stockholm Konsertförening. At first he called it *Fantasia sinfonica*; only later, when it was published, did it become Symphony No. 7.
- It lasts a little over twenty minutes and never stops. The tempo keeps changing, slowly and almost imperceptibly, from a slow beginning to fast dance-like music and back. Three times a noble trombone theme rises above the orchestra, and these three moments are the pillars of the whole work.
- It was his last symphony. He worked on an Eighth for years but destroyed it, and after the tone poem *Tapiola* (1926) he wrote little more of significance in the three decades he still lived.
- In one line: a slow rising, a hymn in the strings and the first trombone theme; a quickening into a storm and the trombone again, in C minor; a dance that speeds up into the third trombone theme, in C major, and a final chord.

### I. Symphony No. 7

**Summary.** One continuous movement. It begins with a slow scale climbing in the strings, settles into a long, hymn-like passage for divided strings, and brings in a majestic trombone theme. The music then speeds up, little by little, into a stormy passage where the trombone returns in the minor; lightens into a dance; speeds up again; and reaches the trombone theme a third time, in full glory. Memories of the opening lead to the final chord.

**Main ideas**

- The rising scale: a slow climb in the strings that opens the symphony.
- The hymn: a long, many-voiced passage for the strings alone, like a chorale.
- The trombone theme: a broad, noble melody for solo trombone, heard three times.
- The dance: a lighter, swinging passage in the middle of the work.

**Things to notice**

1. Try to catch the moment when the tempo changes. Sibelius makes each change gradual, so that you notice only afterwards that the music is moving faster or slower.
2. Listen for the three appearances of the trombone theme: majestic, then stormy, then triumphant. They are the landmarks of the whole symphony.

**In this recording.** Osmo Vänskä and the Lahti Symphony Orchestra recorded all of Sibelius's symphonies for the Swedish label BIS in the 1990s, together with the first recordings of the original versions of the Fifth Symphony and the Violin Concerto. Like all the orchestra's recordings, this one was made in Alvar Aalto's Church of the Cross in Lahti. On the Spotify album the Seventh is track 5, between the Sixth Symphony and *Tapiola*.

### Threads

- **One span.** There are no breaks and no separate movements. Slow, fast and dance-like music flow into each other, and the tempo changes are gradual.
- **The trombone.** One theme, on one instrument, holds the work together: it appears three times, in three moods.
- **C major.** The symphony begins and ends in C major, the plainest of keys; the last chord sounds like an arrival.
- **An ending.** It was Sibelius's last symphony, though he lived until 1957.
