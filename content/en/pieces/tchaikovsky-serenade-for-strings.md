### The big picture

- Tchaikovsky wrote the Serenade in September and October 1880, in the same weeks as the *1812* Overture. He told his patroness Nadezhda von Meck that he had composed it "from an innate impulse", as something he felt deep within himself, and he told his publisher that he loved it "terribly".
- He meant the first movement as an imitation of Mozart's style. Its title, "piece in the form of a sonatina", names a small, classical form.
- It is for strings only: violins, violas, cellos and double basses. In the score Tchaikovsky asked for as many players as possible.
- Teachers and students of the Moscow Conservatory played it to him as a surprise in December 1880. The first public performance was in St Petersburg in October 1881, conducted by Eduard Nápravník. It is dedicated to Karl Albrecht, a cellist who worked at the conservatory.
- In one line: a grand opening and a light-footed dance in Mozart's honour (I), a waltz (II), an elegy (III), two Russian folk songs and a return to the start (IV).

### I. Pezzo in forma di sonatina: Andante non troppo – Allegro moderato

**Summary.** A slow, grand introduction in rich, full chords, then a quick, graceful Allegro. A [[sonatina]] is a small sonata: the themes are laid out and then simply come back, with no development in between. At the end, the grand introduction returns to close the movement.

**Main ideas**

- The introduction: a slow, falling melody in heavy chords, marked *sempre marcatissimo*, always strongly accented.
- Theme 1: a light, dancing tune with little gaps in it.
- Theme 2: quick, scurrying notes passed between the sections.

1. Listen to the opening chords: the violins and violas play two notes at once ([[double stop]]s), which is why the sound is so full.

### II. Waltz: Moderato – Tempo di valse

**Summary.** A light, elegant [[waltz]] that became popular on its own; Tchaikovsky often conducted it separately. The first violins have the tune almost throughout, over the classic "one, two, three" with a heavy bass on the first beat.

**Main ideas**

- The waltz tune: a smooth, swaying melody that climbs and floats down again.
- The middle section: falling phrases in the violins over heavier basses.

**Things to notice**

### III. Élégie: Larghetto elegiaco

**Summary.** An elegy is a song of mourning, but this one is more tender than sad. A slow, mysterious introduction leads to a long melody for the violins over plucked strings. In the middle the music grows darker and more restless; then the melody returns, passed to the violas and then high in the violins.

**Main ideas**

- The introduction: upper strings rising while lower strings fall, in quiet, solemn chords.
- The main melody: warm and singing, first in the violins.

### IV. Finale (Tema russo): Andante – Allegro con spirito

**Summary.** Two Russian folk songs, both of which Tchaikovsky had arranged in a collection years before. A slow introduction on muted strings quotes "On the Green Meadow"; then the Allegro dances off with "Under the Green Apple Tree". Near the end the grand introduction of the first movement returns, and turns into the dance tune.

**Main ideas**

- The introduction: a quiet, hymn-like folk song on muted strings.
- The dance: a quick, bouncing folk tune.
- A second, broad singing tune, first in the cellos.

**Listening stops**

### Threads

- **Mozart.** The first movement imitates Mozart's style, and the serenade itself was a favourite form of Mozart's time.
- **Russia.** The finale is built on two Russian folk songs, which Tchaikovsky had arranged years before.
- **The return.** The grand opening comes back at the end of the first movement and again at the end of the whole work, tying it together.
- **Strings alone.** No winds, no brass, no drums: all the colour comes from bowing, plucking and muting.
