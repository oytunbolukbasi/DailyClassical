### The big picture

- Chopin wrote the sonata in the second half of 1844, much of it during the summer at Nohant, George Sand's country house in central France. It was almost the only work he finished that year; his health was getting worse and he was composing less and less.
- It was published in Paris in 1845 with a dedication to the Countess Élise de Perthuis, whose husband was an aide to King Louis-Philippe.
- It is the last of his piano sonatas, and one of his hardest works to play. Unlike his Second Sonata, with its funeral march, it has a lyrical slow movement and ends in a major key.
- In one line: a grand first movement full of ideas (I), a whirl of fast notes (II), a long night song (III), a galloping finale that ends in triumph (IV).

### I. Allegro maestoso

**Summary.** A proud, falling phrase opens the movement, and one idea grows out of another in quick succession. Then comes one of Chopin's most beautiful melodies, a long song in D major. The movement is in [[sonata form]], with a twist: when the themes return, the opening theme is left out, and the song comes back on its own, now in B major.

**Main ideas**

- Theme 1: a bold, falling phrase in B minor, then a sequence of strong chords.
- Theme 2: a long, calm melody in D major over a gently rocking left hand, like a [[nocturne]].
- The closing theme: soft, rippling figures that end the exposition quietly.

1. Listen for what never comes back: the proud opening phrase is missing from the recapitulation. Chopin did the same in his Second Sonata.

### II. Scherzo: Molto vivace

**Summary.** A very short [[scherzo]]: a whirl of quick, light notes in the right hand, with only a few touches in the left. In the middle, a [[trio]] slows down into a calm, hymn-like tune, then the whirl returns.

**Main ideas**

- The scherzo: a continuous stream of fast notes, light and glittering.
- The trio: a quiet melody in the inner voices, held between long notes above and below.

### III. Largo

**Summary.** After four loud bars in a stern, dotted rhythm, the music becomes a long, calm song in B major, close to a [[nocturne]]. The middle section is much longer than the outer parts: slow, rippling figures in which time seems to stop. The song returns briefly and the movement ends in peace.

**Main ideas**

- The introduction: four forceful bars in a dotted rhythm.
- The song: a slow, singing melody over a quiet, steady accompaniment.
- The middle section: gentle, rippling broken chords in E major, like water at night.

### IV. Finale: Presto non tanto

**Summary.** Eight loud bars in octaves, a pause, and the main theme gallops off over a running bass. The theme returns three times, each time with a faster, fuller accompaniment, between brilliant episodes in other keys. The sonata ends in B major, in triumph.

**Main ideas**

- The introduction: loud octaves in both hands.
- The main theme: an urgent, galloping tune over rolling triplets in the left hand.
- The episodes: brilliant runs and bright major-key music between the returns.

### Threads

- **Song inside the storm.** Every movement holds a song: the D major theme of the first, the trio of the scherzo, the whole Largo.
- **Rules bent.** As in his Second Sonata, Chopin leaves the main theme out of the first movement's recapitulation.
- **From dark to light.** The sonata begins in B minor and ends in B major. His Second Sonata ended in a whisper; this one ends in triumph.
