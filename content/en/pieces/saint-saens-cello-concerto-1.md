### The big picture

- Saint-Saëns finished the concerto in November 1872, when he was 37. He wrote it for the French cellist Auguste Tolbecque, who was also a viola da gamba player and an instrument maker.
- Tolbecque played the premiere on 19 January 1873 at a concert of the Paris Conservatoire's orchestra, the Société des Concerts. Being performed there was a sign that the French musical establishment had accepted Saint-Saëns.
- The concerto is one continuous movement in three sections: fast, then a gentle dance, then fast again. Its themes return and are linked across the sections, so the whole work feels like one sweep.
- It lasts less than twenty minutes, and it has no orchestral introduction: after one chord the cello plunges straight in. The orchestra is kept light, so the cello is heard clearly in every register, and it has remained a favourite of cellists ever since.
- In one line: an agitated, rushing opening (I), a delicate old-fashioned minuet with a singing cello above it (II), the rush returns and turns to light at the end (III).

### I. Allegro non troppo – Allegretto con moto – Tempo primo

**Summary.** The whole concerto plays as one track and one movement, in three linked sections. A fast, agitated first section introduces a rushing cello theme; a delicate minuet for [[muted]] strings follows; then the rushing theme returns, new melodies join it, and the concerto ends brightly in A major.

**Main ideas**

- The rushing theme: falling groups of three quick notes in the cello, agitated and urgent.
- The minuet: a delicate, old-fashioned dance for muted strings, with a long, singing cello line above it.
- The calm melody: a slow, rising line in the cello that ends high and glassy.

**Things to notice**

1. Listen to the very first second. A single chord, and the cello is already playing. There is no orchestral introduction to wait for.
2. In the minuet, notice how far apart the two layers are: the strings dance lightly and quietly, while the cello sings long notes above them.

**In this recording.** Yo-Yo Ma recorded the concerto in Paris in 1980, with Lorin Maazel and the Orchestre National de France. On the Spotify album the concerto is a single track, followed by Lalo's Cello Concerto.

### Threads

- **One movement, three sections.** Saint-Saëns joins fast, slow dance and fast into one continuous piece, a plan that recalls the one-movement works of his friend Liszt.
- **Themes that return.** The rushing opening comes back at the start of the third section, and the end turns the concerto's minor key to major.
- **A light orchestra.** The orchestra is small and the textures are clear, so the cello never has to fight to be heard.
