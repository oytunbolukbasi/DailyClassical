### The big picture

- Wagner wrote the piece in secret in 1870 for his wife Cosima, a year and a half after the birth of their son Siegfried, whom the family called "Fidi". It was first played on Christmas morning, 25 December 1870, which was also Cosima's birthday, by a small group of musicians from Zurich standing on the staircase of the couple's house at Tribschen on Lake Lucerne. Cosima woke to the opening bars.
- Wagner's own title was "Tribschen Idyll, with Fidi's birdsong and the orange sunrise, as a symphonic birthday greeting". The birdsong and the sunrise are thought to refer to private moments of the couple.
- The original band had 13 players: one each of flute, oboe and bassoon, two clarinets, two horns, a trumpet and five strings. The conductor Hans Richter learned the trumpet for its 13 bars; the story goes that he practised in a boat in the middle of the lake, so as not to give the secret away.
- The music is gentle and slow-moving, built from melodies Wagner also used in the last scene of his opera *Siegfried*, and from an old German lullaby, "Schlaf, Kindlein, schlaf".
- Wagner meant it to stay private, but in 1878, short of money, he sold it to a publisher and enlarged the string section. Today it is played by small orchestras as well as by the original 13.

### I. Siegfried Idyll

**Summary.** One long, calm piece in E major, like a morning that slowly brightens. The strings begin very quietly with a rocking, tender melody; the woodwinds join; an oboe sings a lullaby; birds call; the music rises to a glowing climax with horns and the single trumpet, and then sinks back into the peace of the opening.

**Main ideas**

- The opening melody: slow, warm and rocking, in the strings; Wagner later gave it to Brünnhilde in the love duet that ends *Siegfried*.
- The lullaby: a simple, rocking tune on the oboe, from the German cradle song "Schlaf, Kindlein, schlaf".
- Birdsong: quick, chirping calls in the flute and other woodwinds.
- The horn calls: short, open calls in the horns, like Siegfried's horn in the opera.

**Things to notice**

1. Listen for the trumpet. It plays for only a few bars, at the climax, and nowhere else. This was Hans Richter's part on Christmas morning 1870.
2. The oboe's lullaby is a real German cradle song, which Wagner jotted down on New Year's Eve 1868. Notice how calmly it rocks, compared with the warmer, flowing melody of the opening.

**In this recording.** Solti recorded the Idyll in 1965 in the Sofiensaal, Vienna, with players from the Vienna Philharmonic, led by its concertmaster Walter Weller. It was the year the orchestra and Solti were completing their recording of Wagner's *Ring*. On the Spotify album it is track 5, after four overtures.

### Threads

- **A family piece.** A birthday present, a lullaby for a baby, a staircase concert: this is the most private music Wagner wrote.
- **From the opera.** The melodies also live in *Siegfried*, the third opera of *The Ring of the Nibelung*, where they belong to Siegfried and Brünnhilde's love. Here they are about a real son and a real marriage.
- **Small forces.** Eight wind players and five strings: Wagner, famous for enormous orchestras, writes almost like chamber music.
