### The big picture

- *Autumn* is the third of the four concertos known as *The Four Seasons*, published in Amsterdam in 1725 as the first four of the twelve concertos of Vivaldi's Op. 8, *Il cimento dell'armonia e dell'inventione* ("The contest between harmony and invention"). Like the others it was probably written some years earlier.
- Its sonnet, possibly by Vivaldi himself, is about the end of the harvest. The peasants celebrate with songs and dances, and "fired by the liquor of Bacchus", many end their fun in sleep. The air is mild and the season invites everyone to sweet sleep. At dawn the hunters go out with horns, guns and dogs; the animal flees, is wounded and dies.
- It is a concerto in the Baroque way: a refrain for the string band, the [[ritornello]], returns between passages for the solo violin, with the harpsichord and bass instruments, the [[continuo]], underneath. In the first movement the refrain is a simple country dance; in the last, a hunting call.
- The slow movement is unusual: the soloist hardly plays a solo at all. It is a quiet picture of sleep, made of soft, slowly shifting chords.
- In one line: the harvest dance and a drunk who falls asleep (I), everyone asleep (II), the hunt (III).

### I. Allegro

**Summary.** "The peasant celebrates the pleasure of a happy harvest with dances and songs." The band plays a sturdy, foot-stamping country dance. The solo violin joins the party and, as the wine flows, its passages grow wilder: bigger leaps, faster runs, sudden stumbles. Near the end the drunk falls asleep, and the music slows almost to a stop before the dance returns to close.

**Main ideas**

- The harvest dance: a simple, heavy-footed tune for the whole band, with strong accents.
- The drunk: the solo violin, at first playing the dance in chords, then in more and more unsteady runs, trills and leaps.
- The sleep: a slow, soft passage where the music seems to doze off.

### II. Adagio molto

**Summary.** "Everyone gives up dancing and singing; the mild air gives pleasure." The whole village sleeps. Vivaldi wrote "sleeping drunkards" over this movement. There is no real tune: the strings play soft, long chords that slide from one unexpected harmony to the next, while the harpsichord quietly spreads its chords beneath them.

**Main ideas**

- The sleep: very soft, sustained chords in the strings, moving slowly and strangely.
- The harpsichord: gentle rippling chords underneath, the only movement in the music.

### III. La caccia. Allegro

**Summary.** "The hunters set out at the new dawn, with horns, guns and dogs." The band's refrain imitates hunting horns in a bouncing triple rhythm. The solo violin is first the hunt and then the hunted: the animal runs, the guns go off and the dogs bark, and the animal, wounded and exhausted, tries to escape and dies. The horn call returns in triumph.

**Main ideas**

- The hunting call: a bouncing, horn-like tune in the whole band.
- The fleeing animal: fast, running notes in the solo violin.
- The guns and dogs: loud, sudden chords and repeated notes in the band.

**In this recording.** Simon Standage plays all four concertos with The English Concert on period instruments, and Trevor Pinnock directs from the harpsichord, which matters most in the slow movement. *Autumn* is tracks 7–9 of the Spotify album.

### Threads

- **The sonnet.** Every scene follows the poem in order: the harvest dance, the wine, the drunk who falls asleep, the whole village asleep in the mild air, the hunt at dawn, the chase, the death of the animal.
- **Day and night.** The concerto moves from an afternoon party to a night of sleep to the next morning's hunt.
- **The soloist as a character.** The solo violin plays the drunk in the first movement, falls silent with the sleepers in the second, and becomes the hunted animal in the third.
