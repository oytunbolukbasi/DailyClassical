### The big picture

- In 1748 the Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle ended the War of the Austrian Succession, and King George II ordered a huge fireworks display in London's Green Park to celebrate. Handel, settled in London for nearly forty years, was asked for the music. It opened the celebration on 27 April 1749.
- The king wanted only "martial" instruments, wind and drums, and hoped there would be "no fiddles". Handel, who wanted strings, gave way. He wrote for 24 oboes, 12 bassoons and a contrabassoon, 9 trumpets, 9 horns, timpani and side drums, about sixty players.
- Six days earlier a public rehearsal in Vauxhall Gardens drew a crowd said to number twelve thousand, and carriages were stuck for three hours on London Bridge. The fireworks night itself went badly: it rained, many rockets misfired, and one of the wooden pavilions caught fire. The music was the success of the evening.
- A month later Handel performed the suite at the Foundling Hospital, a London home for abandoned children, and added strings. Most performances today use that later version. This recording goes back to the original band, without strings.
- It is a French-style [[suite]]: a grand overture followed by dances. In one line: a grand ceremonial overture (I), a brisk dance (II), a gentle picture of peace (III), a shout of joy (IV), two minuets, one soft and one festive (V).

### I. Ouverture

**Summary.** By far the longest movement, more than a third of the suite. It is a [[French overture]]: a slow, majestic opening in jerky, dotted rhythms, with trumpets and drums, then a fast section of fanfares. A short, slower passage in a minor key interrupts it, and the fast music returns to finish.

**Main ideas**

- The ceremony: slow, grand chords in long-short rhythms, the whole band with trumpets and drums.
- The echoes: phrases answered softly by smaller groups, as if from a distance.
- The fanfares: a fast, bright section built on trumpet calls.

### III. La Paix

**Summary.** "Peace": a slow, rocking movement in the lilting rhythm of a [[siciliano]], the calm centre of the suite. Horns join the oboes and bassoons, and the music gives a gentle, almost pastoral picture of the peace the fireworks celebrated.

**Main ideas**

- The rocking tune: a slow melody in a swaying long-short rhythm, in the oboes.
- The horns: warm, held notes underneath and between the phrases.

### IV. La Réjouissance

**Summary.** "Rejoicing": a short, brilliant movement in D major with all the trumpets and drums. It begins with the trumpets entering one after another on the same call, each a little higher, and the whole tune is played through more than once, each time by a different combination of the band.

**Main ideas**

- The trumpet call: a short rising figure that the three trumpet parts start one after another.
- The full band: oboes, horns, bassoons and drums joining for a festive tutti.

### V. Menuets I and II

**Summary.** The suite ends with two minuets. The first, in D minor, is soft and graceful, for oboes and bassoons. The second, in D major, brings back the trumpets, horns and drums and ends the suite in a festive mood.

**Main ideas**

- Menuet I: a graceful dance in three, in a minor key, without the brass.
- Menuet II: the same kind of dance in a major key, bright and ceremonial.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | A gentle dance in three, in a minor key, oboes over bassoons | The first [[minuet]] |
| ≈ 1:10 | The key brightens to major; horns and trumpets take over | The second minuet |

**Things to notice**

1. Compare the two minuets. They have the same rhythm and nearly the same length; only the key and the instruments change, and the mood changes completely.

**In this recording.** Trevor Pinnock gathered a band the size of Handel's own in 1749: more than fifty wind players and drummers, with no strings, recorded in Watford in 1996.

### Threads

- **Wind and drums.** Without strings, the music is all reeds, brass and percussion, the sound of a military band built for the open air.
- **Loud and soft.** The suite alternates movements with trumpets and drums (I, IV, the second minuet) and gentler ones without them (II, III, the first minuet).
- **A celebration of peace.** Two movements have titles: "La Paix" ("Peace") and "La Réjouissance" ("Rejoicing"). They name the occasion the fireworks celebrated.
