### The big picture

- Ludvig Holberg (1684–1754), born in Bergen, became the great playwright of Denmark–Norway, sometimes called the Molière of the North. Grieg was also from Bergen, and in 1884 the town celebrated Holberg's 200th birthday.
- For the festivities Grieg wrote a cantata for male voices and this suite for piano, which he played himself in December 1884. He then arranged it for string orchestra, and in that form it was first heard in Bergen in March 1885. The string version is the one everyone knows.
- The subtitle is "Suite in the olden style". Grieg borrowed the dances of Holberg's day, the early eighteenth century, and said he had modelled the piece on the French keyboard composers of that time. He joked that it was a "periwig piece". It is an early example of [[neoclassicism]]: old forms, filled with Grieg's own Romantic harmony and Norwegian warmth.
- A Baroque [[suite]] usually opens with a prelude and continues with dances. Grieg keeps that plan, and he lets the string orchestra divide into many parts and solos, so the sound is much richer than any Baroque band.
- In one line: a galloping prelude (I), a stately sarabande (II), a gavotte with a bagpipe musette (III), a sorrowful air (IV), a sparkling rigaudon (V).

### I. Praeludium

**Summary.** The best-known movement: a bright, energetic prelude driven by a galloping rhythm of repeated chords from start to finish. In the piano original this was a stream of broken chords; for strings Grieg turned it into this pulsing pattern, with a broad, bold tune singing out beneath it.

**Main ideas**

- The gallop: repeated chords in a long-short-short rhythm, passed through the upper strings.
- The tune: a broad, rising melody in the lower strings, under the gallop.

### II. Sarabande

**Summary.** A slow, dignified [[sarabande]], the old Spanish-French dance in three beats. Grieg divides the strings into many parts, so the chords sound thick and glowing. In the middle a solo cello comes forward, warm and singing.

**Main ideas**

- The dance: a noble melody in slow triple time, with the weight often on the second beat.
- The cello's song: a solo cello leads in the middle section, richer and darker.

### III. Gavotte – Musette

**Summary.** Two French dances in one. A graceful [[gavotte]] frames a [[musette]], a country dance named after a small French bagpipe: under its tune the low strings hold a long, buzzing note, as a bagpipe's drone would.

**Main ideas**

- The gavotte: a poised, courtly tune in G major.
- The musette: a quicker, rustic tune in C major over a [[drone]].

### IV. Air

**Summary.** The emotional heart of the suite: a slow, grave song in G minor, marked "Andante religioso". The form is Baroque, but the dark, sometimes feverish harmony and the long, sighing lines are Grieg's own.

**Main ideas**

- The air: a long, sorrowful melody in the violins over a gently pulsing accompaniment.
- The climax: the melody climbs and the harmony grows more intense, almost passionate.

### V. Rigaudon

**Summary.** A quick, cheerful [[rigaudon]], an old French dance in two. A solo violin and a solo viola race along together while the rest of the strings accompany them with plucked chords. A slower, more wistful middle section in G minor gives contrast before the dance returns.

**Main ideas**

- The rigaudon: a bright, leaping tune in duet for solo violin and solo viola.
- The middle section: a calmer, singing tune in G minor for the full strings.

**In this recording.** Marriner and the Academy recorded the suite in May 1970 in London's Kingsway Hall, and the first LP paired it with Dvořák's Serenade for Strings.

### Threads

- **Old forms, new feelings.** Each movement borrows an eighteenth-century dance, but the harmony, especially in the Air, belongs to Grieg's own time.
- **Bergen's two sons.** Grieg wrote it to honour a writer from his own city, a century and a half older.
- **A string orchestra of many voices.** Grieg divides the sections, gives solos to single players and mixes bowed and plucked sounds, so a small band sounds like a full orchestra.
