### The big picture

- In 1797 Goethe wrote a ballad, "Der Zauberlehrling" ("The Sorcerer's Apprentice"). An old sorcerer goes out; his apprentice, tired of carrying water, uses a spell he has overheard to make a broom fetch it. He has forgotten the word that stops it. The house floods, he splits the broom with an axe, and now two brooms carry water, twice as fast, until the master comes back and breaks the spell. The story is much older: around AD 150 the Greek writer Lucian told it with a pestle instead of a broom.
- Paul Dukas wrote his piece exactly a century after the poem and called it a [[scherzo]] "after a ballad by Goethe". He conducted the first performance himself, in Paris on 18 May 1897, at a concert of the Société Nationale de Musique. It was customary to print Goethe's poem in the score.
- It is [[programme music]] that follows the poem closely, event by event. You do not need the words: the bassoons play the broom, the brass the magic, and the orchestra the rising water.
- It became by far Dukas's best-known work, and its popularity came to irritate him. In 1940 Walt Disney used it in *Fantasia*, with Leopold Stokowski conducting and Mickey Mouse as the apprentice.
- In one line: the silent workshop, the spell, the broom at work, the flood, the axe, two brooms and a worse flood, the master's return.

### I. Assez lent – Vif – Assez lent

**Summary.** One movement that tells the story in order. A slow, mysterious introduction sets the scene and hints at every tune to come. Then the spell, and the broom starts to walk, in the bassoons. The tune is passed around the orchestra and grows louder and wilder until the axe falls. A short silence, the pieces start moving again, and the second flood is bigger than the first. The master's command cuts it off, the opening music returns, and four quick blows end the piece.

**Main ideas**

- The broom: a perky, rather clumsy tune in short, detached notes, first in the bassoons over plucked strings. It climbs a few steps and keeps hammering the home note.
- The apprentice: a lighter, eager tune in the woodwinds, decorated with quick grace notes.
- The spell: a loud call in dotted rhythm for the brass, played with mutes, so that it snarls.

**Things to notice**

1. Follow the bassoons. In the opening minutes they are the broom, and their short, stubborn notes give it a personality: obedient, tireless and a little stupid.
2. Listen to the silence after the axe. Dukas lets it last long enough for you to think the trouble is over, and then the scraps of tune start to move again in the lowest instruments.

**In this recording.** James Levine recorded the piece with the Berlin Philharmonic in 1986, for an album with Saint-Saëns's Organ Symphony; on the Spotify album it is track 5. Thirteen years later Levine conducted the new music for Disney's *Fantasia 2000*, the sequel that kept Mickey Mouse's *Sorcerer's Apprentice* from the original film.

### Threads

- **A ballad without words.** Every event of Goethe's poem has its sound: the spell in the brass, the broom in the bassoons, the water in the strings and harp, the axe as two chords and a silence.
- **The same tune, wilder and wilder.** Almost the whole piece is built from the broom's short tune. It begins slow and half-asleep in the introduction and is then repeated, louder, higher and in more instruments, until it fills the orchestra.
- **A scherzo, a joke.** *Scherzo* means "joke" in Italian. The story is a warning, but Dukas tells it with a smile: clumsy bassoons, a glockenspiel, and four smacks at the end.
