      Bizet was born in Paris in 1838. He was registered as Alexandre César Léopold but baptised Georges, the name he kept. His father, a former wigmaker, had become a singing teacher, and his mother was a fine pianist. The boy was so gifted that the Paris Conservatoire took him at nine, below the official age. He won first prize for piano, studied composition with Fromental Halévy and became a friend of the young Camille Saint-Saëns. At seventeen he wrote a Symphony in C full of youthful sparkle, but never published it; it was found again in 1933 and first played in 1935.

      In 1857 he won the Prix de Rome and spent nearly three years at the Villa Medici in Rome. Back in Paris, success would not come. The opera houses preferred established names, and he earned his living largely by arranging other composers' music. Neither *The Pearl Fishers* (1863) nor *The Fair Maid of Perth* (1867) succeeded at first. In 1869 he married Geneviève Halévy, his late teacher's daughter; their only son, Jacques, was born in 1872. Its realism and its heroine, who lives and dies by her own rules, shocked the audience, and Bizet believed it had failed. Three months later, on 3 June 1875, his sixth wedding anniversary, he died of a heart attack at Bougival, near Paris, aged 36. Within a few years *Carmen* was being performed all over the world, and it is now one of the most popular operas ever written.

## Georges Bizet (added 2026-11-17, batch November 2026, group I)
- Facts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Bizet · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen
- Born 25 October 1838, Paris; registered as Alexandre César Léopold, baptised "Georges" 16 March 1840. Father Adolphe Bizet, hairdresser and wigmaker turned singing teacher; mother Aimée Delsarte, accomplished pianist. Entered the Conservatoire aged nine (minimum age 10 waived after an interview with Joseph Meifred). Piano with Marmontel (second prize 1851, first prize 1852); composition with Fromental Halévy from 1853; friendship with Saint-Saëns (met as a fellow pupil of Gounod's circle). Symphony in C written in 1855 shortly after his 17th birthday, never published, rediscovered 1933, first performed 1935.
- Prix de Rome 1857; Villa Medici from 27 January 1858 (Rome 1858–1860, "almost three years in Italy"). Paris theatres preferred the established repertoire; "earned his living mainly by arranging and transcribing the music of others". *Les pêcheurs de perles*, Théâtre Lyrique, 30 September 1863 (lukewarm, 18 performances); *La jolie fille de Perth* (1867) not immediately successful. Married Geneviève Halévy (daughter of Fromental Halévy, who died 1862) in 1869 (3 June, since he died on his wedding anniversary). Son Jacques born 10 July 1872. *L'Arlésienne* (Daudet), Vaudeville, 1 October 1872, music dismissed; four-movement suite under Pasdeloup, 10 November 1872, enthusiastic reception.
- *Carmen*, Opéra-Comique, 3 March 1875; Chevalier of the Legion of Honour announced that morning; Bizet convinced it had failed. Died 3 June 1875 at Bougival (holiday home), "the early hours of 3 June, his wedding anniversary", heart attack ("a cardiac complication of acute articular rheumatism"), aged 36. "Died of a heart attack three months later, unaware that it would prove a spectacular and enduring success."
- Symphonies: Symphony in C (1855) and *Roma* (performed under Pasdeloup, 28 February 1869). Card: "One, the Symphony in C, and the symphony *Roma*".
- Portrait: Étienne Carjat (1828–1906), photograph, 1875; published as the frontispiece of Schirmer's 1895 *Carmen* vocal score and in The Strand Musical Magazine (1896).
