Pairing note (in the drafts): Turner sailed to Staffa in 1831, two years after Mendelssohn, and showed this stormy view of the island at the Royal Academy in 1832, the year the overture was first played in London.

**Alternative 1.** Thomas Moran, *Fingal's Cave, Island of Staffa, Scotland*, 1884–85. High Museum of Art, Atlanta. Commons: `File:Fingal’s Cave, Island of Staffa, Scotland by Thomas Moran, 1884-5, High Museum of Art.jpg` (929 × 768, public domain; too small for the hero image, look for the museum's own scan). Pairing: the basalt columns and the sea surging into the cave that Klingemann described.

**Alternative 2.** Vilhelm Melbye, *Fingal's Cave with Part of the South of Staffa, Gathering Storm* (*Fingals Hule … Optrækkende Uveir*), 1851. Private collection (sold at Bruun Rasmussen). Commons: `File:Vilhelm Melbye - Fingals Hule med et Parti af den sydlige Deel af Øen Staffa. Optrækkende Uveir - 1851.png` (688 × 433, public domain; far too small, listed for the subject only). Pairing: Staffa under an approaching storm, the overture's sea and weather.

Also relevant but not a painting: Mendelssohn's own pencil drawing *Ein Blick auf die Hebriden und Morven* (7 August 1829, Bodleian Library), made the day he wrote down the opening bars.
