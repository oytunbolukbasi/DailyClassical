Pairing note (in the drafts): a great public fireworks display of the 18th century, rockets bursting over a castle at night in clouds of smoke, the kind of spectacle Handel's music was written to open.

**Alternative 1.** Canaletto, *The Old Horse Guards from St James's Park*, c. 1749. Private collection (on long-term loan; check the current holder before use). Commons: `File:Canaletto_-_A_View_of_the_Old_Horse_Guards_and_Banqueting_Hall,_from_St_James's_Park.jpg` (2000 × 1226, public domain; larger files on Commons are gallery photos tagged CC BY 3.0). Pairing: London in the very year of the fireworks, the royal park next to Green Park. No fireworks in the picture.

**Alternative 2.** Nicolas-Jean-Baptiste Raguenet, *Fête on the Place de Grève for the Birth of Princess Marie-Thérèse* (decorations for the fireworks), 1746. Musée Carnavalet, Paris. Commons: `File:Nicolas-Jean-Baptiste_Raguenet,_Place_de_Grève_et_décorations_pour_le_feu_d'artifice.jpg` (8032 × 4451, PD-old-100; Paris Musées open access). Pairing: a royal fireworks celebration three years before Handel's, with the temporary fireworks building in the square, the kind of structure Servandoni (also a Paris designer) built in Green Park. The fireworks building is small in the picture and nothing is burning yet.

(Period images of the 1749 Green Park display itself exist only as prints, e.g. Paul Angier, *A Perspective View of the Building for the Fireworks in the Green Park*, 1749, YCBA B1994.4.8, CC0, 1920 × 1189; Rijksmuseum RP-P-OB-84.057, CC0, 5256 × 4608. Not paintings, so not proposed.)

## Handel (added 2026-11-17, batch November 2026, group B)
- Facts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Frideric_Handel · https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q7302 (born 23 February 1685 O.S. / 5 March N.S.; died 14 April 1759)
- Born in Halle (Duchy of Magdeburg, Saxony region); the card gives the Old Style date, as for Bach, and says so. Died 14 April 1759 at home in Brook Street, London, aged 74; state funeral, buried in Westminster Abbey.
- Father an "eminent barber-surgeon" serving the court of Saxe-Weissenfels; matriculated at the University of Halle on 10 February 1702 (the law wish is the traditional account in the Wikipedia biography). Hamburg opera orchestra (violin, harpsichord); *Almira* and *Nero* produced 1705. Italy (about 1706–1710; Rome, sacred music while opera was banned in the Papal States). Kapellmeister to George, Elector of Hanover, June 1710, left at the end of the year; *Rinaldo* (1711) a great success; settled in London 1712; the Elector became George I in 1714. *Water Music* on the Thames, July 1717. Royal Academy of Music (opera company) 1719; naturalised 1727; *Zadok the Priest* (one of four coronation anthems for George II, 1727) "performed at every British coronation since 1727". 1737 breakdown, turn to English oratorio; *Messiah* composed August–September 1741, first performed Dublin 13 April 1742; Foundling Hospital benefit performances from 1750, copy bequeathed. Cataract from 1751, "completely blind by 1752". All from the Wikipedia biography (lead and Life sections, read 2026-11-17).
- Nationality "German-British": Wikipedia lead ("a German-British Baroque composer").
- Portrait: Thomas Hudson (1701–1779), *George Frideric Handel*, 1756, oil on canvas, National Portrait Gallery, London (NPG 8).
