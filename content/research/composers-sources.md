# Composer sheet: sources (checked 2026-10-04)

Facts, bios and portraits in `content/composers.yaml`. Birth and death dates and places were cross-checked
against Wikidata (P569/P570/P19/P20) and the English Wikipedia biography of each composer. Bio claims
come from the same Wikipedia articles and Encyclopaedia Britannica's entries; nothing in the bios goes
beyond what those sources state. Portrait licences were read from the Wikimedia Commons API
(`prop=imageinfo&iiprop=url|size|extmetadata`) and the file pages.

Q1151 (Berlioz), Q7294 (Brahms), Q7315 (Tchaikovsky), Q7298 (Dvořák), Q7304 (Mahler), Q80135 (Shostakovich).

## Mozart
- Facts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart · https://www.britannica.com/biography/Wolfgang-Amadeus-Mozart · https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q254
- "41 numbered": the traditional numbering runs to No. 41 (*Jupiter*); several numbered works are spurious and some unnumbered ones are authentic, so the card says "numbered" rather than giving a total.
- Last three symphonies written in summer 1788 (June–August); *Requiem* completed by Süssmayr.
- Portrait: Barbara Krafft (1764–1825), posthumous portrait, 1819, oil on canvas, Gesellschaft der Musikfreunde, Vienna.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Barbara_Krafft_-_Portr%C3%A4t_Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart_(1819).jpg — 1447 × 1784, Public domain (PD-Art, life + 100).

## Schubert
- Facts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Schubert · https://www.britannica.com/biography/Franz-Schubert · https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q7312

## Mahler
- Facts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Mahler · https://www.britannica.com/biography/Gustav-Mahler · https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q7304
- Fourteen children, many died in infancy. Vienna Conservatory 1875. Conversion February 1897; Vienna Court Opera 1897–1907. "The symphony must be like the world. It must embrace everything" is reported by Sibelius from their 1907 meeting, hence "is said to have told Sibelius". Met Opera and New York Philharmonic 1908–1911. Nationality: born in Bohemia (Austrian Empire), worked as an Austrian; shown as "Austrian".
- Portrait: Akseli Gallen-Kallela (1865–1931), *Portrait of Gustav Mahler*, 1907, Gösta Serlachius Fine Arts Foundation (Serlachius Museums), Mänttä.

## Anton Bruckner (added 2026-10, batch group D)
- Facts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Bruckner · https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Bruckner · https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q81752 (P569 1824-09-04, P570 1896-10-11, P19 Ansfelden, P20 Vienna)
- Choirboy at St Florian after his father's death (1837); organist at St Florian from 1848; Linz cathedral organist from 8 December 1855 (de.wikipedia); studies with Sechter from 1855, mostly by correspondence; composing seriously from 1861 (aged 37) after lessons with Otto Kitzler, who introduced him to Wagner; Vienna Conservatory 1868; organ tours to France 1869 and England 1871.
- Third Symphony dedicated to Wagner; its 1877 premiere his greatest failure; Hanslick's hostility (de.wikipedia). Breakthrough with the Seventh, Leipzig 1884. Ninth unfinished. Buried at St Florian below the organ.
- "Nine numbered (the Ninth unfinished), plus two early ones": Wikipedia counts eleven symphonies including the F minor Study Symphony (1863) and the unnumbered D minor symphony.
- Portrait: Ferry Bératon (1859–1900), *Anton Bruckner*, 1889, oil on canvas, 85.6 × 75.8 cm, Wien Museum inv. 16837 (https://sammlung.wienmuseum.at/objekt/42817-anton-bruckner-1824-1896-komponist-und-organist/).

## Franz Liszt (added 2026-10-10, batch G)
- Facts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Liszt · https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q41309 (P569 1811-10-22, P570 1886-07-31, P19 Raiding Q660821, P20 Bayreuth Q3923)
- Born in Doborján (now Raiding, Austria), then in the Kingdom of Hungary; father a land steward for Prince Nikolaus II Esterházy. Studied with Czerny (piano) and Salieri (composition) in Vienna; settled in Paris 1823. Heard Paganini in 1832 and resolved to match his virtuosity. Gave the solo recital its modern form. Heine coined "Lisztomania" (1844). Daughter Cosima (with Marie d'Agoult) married Wagner. Weimar court Kapellmeister from 1848 (resigned 1858, left 1859); Princess Carolyne zu Sayn-Wittgenstein; premiered *Lohengrin* 1850; thirteen symphonic poems, the first twelve 1848–58. Minor orders 1865 ("Abbé Liszt"). From 1872 divided time between Rome, Weimar and Budapest; helped establish the Budapest academy that bears his name. "Liszt did not charge for lessons"; pupil estimates run to over 400. Late works experimental, anticipating 20th-century developments.
- "Toured widely": Wikipedia lead ("He toured widely").
- Symphonies: *A Faust Symphony* and the *Dante Symphony* (Wikipedia, list of works; the Dante Symphony was not in the fetched excerpt — standard).
- Nationality "Hungarian": Liszt called himself Hungarian; Wikipedia's lead: "a Hungarian composer".
- Portrait: Henri Lehmann (1814–1882), *Portrait of Franz Liszt*, 1839, oil on canvas, Musée Carnavalet, Paris (Commons category "Franz Liszt (Henri Lehmann - Musée Carnavalet)", "Images from Paris Musées").
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Liszt_(Lehmann_portrait).jpg — 4760 × 6256, {{PD-Art|PD-old-auto-expired|deathyear=1882}}. focal_y 0.15 (face at about 20 % from the top; checked on a thumbnail). A square crop exists as File:Liszt_(Lehmann_portrait)_(cropped).jpg (3860 × 3862).

## Edvard Grieg (added 2026-10-10, batch G)
- Facts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Grieg · https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q80621 (P569 1843-06-15, P570 1907-09-04, P19/P20 Bergen Q26793)
- Mother Gesine Judithe Hagerup, a music teacher, his first piano teacher from the age of six. Ole Bull persuaded the parents to send the 15-year-old to the Leipzig Conservatory (1858). 1860: pleurisy and tuberculosis; "destroyed left lung" and a spinal deformity for life. Copenhagen for three years from 1863; friendship with Rikard Nordraak, who died in 1866; Grieg wrote a funeral march in his honour. Married his first cousin, the lyric soprano Nina Hagerup, on 11 June 1867. Incidental music for Ibsen's *Peer Gynt* ("Morning Mood", "In the Hall of the Mountain King"). "Sixty-six Lyric Pieces for piano in ten books" (Opp. 12–71). Suppressed an early symphony. Died in Bergen, 4 September 1907, of heart failure. Troldhaugen is now the Edvard Grieg Museum; his ashes were placed in a mountain crypt near Troldhaugen, Nina's later alongside.

## Joaquín Rodrigo (added 2026-11-17, batch 2026-11, group J)
- Facts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaqu%C3%ADn_Rodrigo · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concierto_de_Aranjuez · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Kamhi · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C3%BCksel_Koptagel
- Born 22 November 1901, Sagunto (province of Valencia); died at home in Madrid, 6 July 1999, aged 97 (Wikipedia lead and "Life"). Lost his sight completely at three after diphtheria (Wikipedia; some notes say "almost completely": the bio says "almost completely", as the concerto article does: "nearly blind since age three"). Music from eight (solfège, piano, violin), harmony and composition from 16; teacher in Valencia Francisco Antich; wrote in Braille, transcribed for publication.
- Paris: École Normale de Musique with Paul Dukas; 1927 (runyanprogramnotes.com: "In 1927 he moved to Paris and became a composition student of Paul Dukas"; Hollywood Bowl note: "moved to Paris in 1927 to study with Paul Dukas").
- Victoria Kamhi: Turkish pianist of Sephardic Jewish family, born in Istanbul (en.wikipedia gives 1905; birth year not used), met Rodrigo in 1929, married 19 January 1933; "lived in France and Germany before returning to Spain in 1939"; she left her career as a pianist and was his assistant; memoir *De la mano de Joaquín Rodrigo* (English 1992); died 21 July 1997; both buried in Aranjuez.
- Concierto de Aranjuez: Paris, early 1939; for Regino Sainz de la Maza, at his suggestion (Hollywood Bowl / LA Phil note: https://www.hollywoodbowl.com/musicdb/pieces/269/concierto-de-aranjuez); premiere Barcelona, 9 November 1940. "Almost overnight" fame: Wikipedia ("its success established his reputation"); BHSO note: "immediate success" (https://www.bhso.org.uk/work/rodrigo-concierto-de-aranjuez/).
- From 1947 professor of music history, Manuel de Falla Chair of Music, Faculty of Philosophy and Letters, Complutense University of Madrid; "Notable students include Yüksel Koptagel, Turkish composer and pianist" (Koptagel article: "studied in the Madrid Royal Conservatory with José Cubiles, Joaquin Rodrigo"; the two articles differ on the institution, so the bio says only "among his students").
- Later works: *Fantasía para un gentilhombre* (1954) at the request of Andrés Segovia; *Concierto serenata* for Zabaleta; *Concierto pastoral* for James Galway; *Concierto como un divertimento* for Julian Lloyd Webber; *Concierto andaluz* commissioned by Celedonio Romero for himself and his three sons.
- "Stayed close to tradition" / Aranjuez overshadowed his other works: BHSO note (style blends mainstream European classicism with Iberian harmony; the concerto made him famous and "he was never able to meet the high expectations"); MusicWeb programme note quoting Julian Bream: "The success of Concierto de Aranjuez has somehow eclipsed Rodrigo's other works" (https://musicweb-international.com/programme_notes/rodrigo_aranjuez.htm).
- Honours: Premio Nacional de Música 1983; 30 December 1991 Marqués de los Jardines de Aranjuez (King Juan Carlos I); Prince of Asturias Award (Arts) 1996; Commander of Arts and Letters 1998. Daughter Cecilia (b. 27 January 1941) succeeded to the title.
- Symphonies: none (works list on Wikipedia has orchestral pieces and concertos, no symphony).
- Portrait: `null`. Checked Commons `Category:Joaquín Rodrigo` and searches (2026-11-17): the only images are photos of monuments (Aranjuez, Sagunto, Santa Cruz de Tenerife), a bust in Rosario, Argentina (`File:Joaquin_Rodrigo_en_Rosario.JPG`, the en/es Wikipedia infobox image: a photo of a 1999 sculpture, not a portrait, and the sculpture's own rights are not cleared), plaques, his grave, his coat of arms, and a 354 × 260 scan of a certificate.
