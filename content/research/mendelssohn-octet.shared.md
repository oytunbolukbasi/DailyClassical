**Alternative 1.** Henry Fuseli (Johann Heinrich Füssli, 1741–1825), *Titania Caresses the Ass-Headed Bottom* (*Titania liebkost den eselköpfigen Bottom*), 1793–94, Kunsthaus Zürich. Commons: `File:Füssli, Johann Heinrich - Titania liebkost den eselköpfigen Bottom - hi res.jpg` (3908 × 4848, portrait, PD-Art; **a book scan, "enlarged"**: check for halftone before use). Pairing: the fairy queen Titania; Goethe's "Walpurgis Night's Dream", thought to lie behind the Octet's scherzo, is subtitled "Oberon and Titania's Golden Wedding", and Fuseli died in 1825, the year of the Octet. Stronger idea, weaker image.

**Alternative 2.** Karl Friedrich Schinkel (1781–1841), *The Hall of Stars in the Palace of the Queen of the Night* (stage design for *The Magic Flute*), c. 1815, Kupferstichkabinett, Staatliche Museen zu Berlin. Commons: `File:Karl Friedrich Schinkel - Die Sternenhalle der Königin der Nacht (ca. 1815).jpg` (8033 × 5955, Google Arts & Culture, PD-Art). Pairing: a night sky of stars from the Berlin stage of Mendelssohn's childhood, as airy and magical as the scherzo. Gouache, not oil.
