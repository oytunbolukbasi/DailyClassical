## 1. Reference recording: The Tallis Scholars / Peter Phillips (Gimell, 1985)

Why this one: the best-known recording of the motet, by the ensemble named after the composer, forty singers one to a part.

**Which Spotify album.** Gimell's original album, "Thomas Tallis – Spem in Alium" (Spotify 69n01dGwqZ5d5b1GITfP7G, 1985-10-01, 26 tracks), **splits the motet into four tracks** (4:58 "Spem in Alium" / 2:25 "in Tribulatione Dimittis" / 1:00 "Respice" / 1:28 "Respice (Repeat)"), so it cannot be the reference (one movement must be one track). The reference is Gimell's later collection *The Tallis Scholars sing Thomas Tallis* (CDGIM 203), where the motet is a single track.

**Portrait: none.** The only image on Commons (File:Thomas Tallis 001.jpg, 1964 × 2533, an 18th-century engraving by Niccolò Haym after Gerard van der Gucht, reproduced in *The Musical Times* 1913) is an imaginary likeness made some 150 years after Tallis's death. A clearly labelled posthumous engraving could be used ("imaginary portrait, c. 1730"), but it would show a face that is not Tallis's in the app's cover crop, so the sheet sets `portrait: null` and the app omits the picture.
