Pairing note (in the drafts): a French princess dressed as a Turkish lady by a painter who had lived in Constantinople (1738–42); Mozart's finale does the same in sound, putting European music into "Turkish" costume.

**Alternative 1.** Antoine de Favray, *Portrait of the Comtesse de Vergennes in Turkish Attire*, c. 1766. Pera Museum, Istanbul. Commons: `File:Vergennes Kontesi’nin Türk Giysileri İçinde Portresi.png` (Favray died 1798, public domain; check resolution before use). Pairing: the wife of the French ambassador to the Porte in Ottoman dress, painted in Istanbul a decade before the concerto; the same European "Turkish" fashion, seen from the inside.

**Alternative 2.** Jean-Étienne Liotard, *A Frankish Woman and Her Servant* (also catalogued as *A Lady in Turkish Dress and Her Servant*), c. 1750. Commons: `File:Jean-Etienne_Liotard_-_A_Lady_in_Turkish_Dress_and_Her_Servant_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg` (public domain; holding museum to confirm from the Commons page, a version is at the Nelson-Atkins Museum of Art). Pairing: a European woman in Levantine dress; turquerie as everyday fashion.

## 2. Glossary

EN:

| Term | Short | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alla turca | In the "Turkish" style, imitating Janissary bands | Italian for "in the Turkish manner": music that imitates, often only distantly, the Ottoman Janissary bands (mehter), with a march rhythm, loud unison tunes, repeated figures and the sound of drums and cymbals. It was a European fashion of the 18th century, not real Ottoman music. |
