## Ottorino Respighi (added 2026-11-17, batch 2026-11, group J)
- Facts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottorino_Respighi · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pines_of_Rome
- Born 9 July 1879, 8 Via Guido Reni, Bologna; father Giuseppe, a postal worker and accomplished pianist who taught at the Accademia Filarmonica di Bologna; first piano and violin lessons from his father. Liceo Musicale di Bologna from 1891 (violin and viola with Federico Sarti; composition with Martucci, then Torchi); violin diploma June 1899. Winter 1900: principal violist, Russian Imperial Theatre, St Petersburg; five months of lessons in orchestration and composition with Rimsky-Korsakov (again in 1902). Final composition exam June 1901 (*Preludio, corale e fuga*); Martucci: "Respighi is not a pupil, Respighi is a master." Read literature in eleven languages.
- 1900s: violinist at the Teatro Comunale and in Mugellini's quintet; first transcription 1906, Monteverdi *Lamento d'Arianna* a success in Berlin 1908. January 1913: professor of composition, Liceo Musicale di Santa Cecilia, Rome. Pupil Elsa Olivieri-Sangiacomo, married January 1919. *Fountains of Rome*: premiere March 1917, lukewarm response; Toscanini's Milan concerts, February 1918, made him one of the leading Italian composers. *Ancient Airs and Dances* Suite 1 premiered December 1917 (free transcriptions of 16th-century lute pieces). *La Boutique fantasque* for Diaghilev, from Rossini's *Péchés de vieillesse* (commissioned 1916, staged 1919).
- 1923 first director of the state Conservatorio di Santa Cecilia, resigned 1926, taught an advanced composition course until 1935. *Pines of Rome* December 1924; first US visit December 1925; Brazil 1927–28; *Roman Festivals* premiered by Toscanini in New York, 21 February 1929. "Steered a neutral course" towards the Fascist government, which exploited his music; Reale Accademia d'Italia 1932. Ill at the end of 1935 while writing *Lucrezia*; subacute bacterial endocarditis diagnosed January 1936; died 18 April 1936 in Rome, aged 56. Elsa outlived him "for almost 60 years" (died 1996).
- Symphonies: one, *Sinfonia drammatica* (1914) (Wikipedia list of compositions; the bio does not mention it).
