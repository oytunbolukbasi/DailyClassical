## Samuel Barber (added 2026-10-24)
- Facts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Barber · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adagio_for_Strings · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_Quartet_(Barber)
- Born 9 March 1910, West Chester, Pennsylvania; father Samuel Le Roy Barber, physician; mother Marguerite, pianist. Aunt Louise Homer, leading contralto at the Metropolitan Opera; uncle Sidney Homer, composer of American art songs, mentor for more than 25 years. First piece (*Sadness*) at seven; letter to his mother at nine ("I was not meant to be an athlet [sic]. I was meant to be a composer"). Organist at a local church at 12. Curtis Institute from age 14 (youth programme), graduated 1934; studied composition (Scalero), piano, conducting (Reiner), voice; met Menotti in 1928, partner for more than 40 years. Professional baritone briefly; weekly NBC radio contract 1935–36. Rome Prize (American Academy in Rome, 1935–37); Symphony in One Movement premiered in Rome, December 1936, first American symphonic work at the Salzburg Festival (1937). Adagio for Strings, Toscanini, 1938. Army Air Corps 1942–45. Capricorn, Mount Kisco, bought 1943 with Menotti, home until 1972. *Knoxville: Summer of 1915* (1947/48, Eleanor Steber). Pulitzer Prizes: *Vanessa* (Met premiere January 1958) and the Piano Concerto (written for the opening of Lincoln Center's Philharmonic Hall, September 1962). *Antony and Cleopatra* opened the new Metropolitan Opera House in 1966, poorly received; depression and alcoholism afterwards. Second Symphony later suppressed. Died of cancer 23 January 1981 at his Fifth Avenue apartment, Manhattan, aged 70; buried in Oaklands Cemetery, West Chester.
