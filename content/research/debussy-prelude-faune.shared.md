**Alternative 1.** Camille Corot (1796–1875), *Une matinée. La danse des nymphes* (*A Morning. The Dance of the Nymphs*), c. 1850, oil on canvas, 98 × 131 cm, Musée d'Orsay, Paris (Commons gives RF 1783; another Commons file gives RF 73: check). Commons: `File:Camille Corot - A Morning. The Dance of the Nymphs - Google Art Project.jpg`, 5442 × 4063, {{PD-Art|PD-old-100-1923}}. Pairing: nymphs dancing in a hazy French landscape, the dreamlike, softly lit mood of Debussy's score (morning, not afternoon).

**Alternative 2.** Unknown maker, School of Fontainebleau, *Pan cuts the reed into which the nymph Syrinx has turned*, mid-16th century, J. Paul Getty Museum, Los Angeles. Commons: `File:Unknown maker, School of Fontainebleau - Pan cuts the reed into which the nymph Syrinx has turned - Google Art Project.jpg`, 3186 × 4395, PD. Pairing: the myth of the pan-pipes, cut from the reed the nymph became, which Mallarmé's faun recalls.

## 2. Glossary

EN:

| Term | Short | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole-tone scale | A six-note scale of equal steps, with no home note | A scale made only of whole steps, six notes to the octave. Because every step is the same size, no note sounds like home, which gives the music a floating, dreamlike quality. Debussy used it often. |
