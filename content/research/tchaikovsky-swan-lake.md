### Why these six numbers

The brief asks for five to seven numbers telling the story from Act 1 to the finale, one track each. Chosen: the Act 1 Waltz (the party, the best-known number of Act 1, Suite Op. 20a No. 2); No. 10 (the swan theme at the lake, Suite No. 1); No. 13 (all the Act 2 dances, including the little swans and the love duet; on this album one track); one Act 3 character dance (the Neapolitan Dance: a solo cornet and a tarantella, easy to hear, short); No. 24 (the betrayal, the swan theme in the brass, the dramatic end of Act 3); No. 29 (storm and the swan theme in the major). Not chosen: Act 1 No. 9 (the swan theme also appears in II and is the same idea), Act 2 No. 14 (repeats No. 10's music), the Czárdás/Spanish/Mazurka (similar function to IV), Act 4 Nos. 25–28 (No. 29 carries the ending). Six numbers keep the total at 41 minutes.
