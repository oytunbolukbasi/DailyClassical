### The big picture

- Shostakovich wrote the concerto in early 1957 for his son Maxim, a piano student at the Moscow Conservatory. Maxim gave the first performance on 10 May 1957, his nineteenth birthday, at his graduation concert.
- It was written to be playable by a gifted young pianist, like his earlier Concertino for two pianos. Cheerful music is rare in Shostakovich, and this is among his sunniest works.
- He was dismissive of it. A week after finishing it he wrote to a younger composer that it had "no redeeming artistic merits". Writers have suggested that, after years of official criticism, he was getting his own disparagement in first.
- Listeners disagreed with him from the start. It is one of his most popular pieces, and its first movement became the animated tale of *The Steadfast Tin Soldier* in Disney's *Fantasia 2000* (1999).
- The orchestra has no trumpets or trombones: woodwind, four horns, timpani, snare drum and strings.
- In one line: a perky march (I), a quiet, tender song (II), a dance with a joke about piano exercises (III).

### I. Allegro

**Summary.** A bright, busy [[sonata form]] that begins with a bassoon and builds to a march. The piano's first tune is simple enough for a beginner, both hands an octave apart, but the movement grows more brilliant as it goes, with a solo [[cadenza]] in which the piano plays several voices at once.

**Main ideas**

- The bassoon tune: perky and marching, the orchestra's theme.
- The piano tune: a plain, quick line played in octaves, which turns into a march.
- The D minor tune: darker, in bare octaves on the piano.

### II. Andante

**Summary.** The heart of the concerto: a slow, tender song. The strings begin alone in C minor; the piano enters in a gentle C major with a melody whose accompaniment moves in threes against the strings' twos. The movement does not end but flows straight into the finale.

**Main ideas**

- The string introduction: slow, hushed and a little sad.
- The piano's song: a simple, songlike melody, calm and unhurried.

### III. Allegro

**Summary.** A dance in two beats that dashes along on folk-like five-note scales, with a second tune in a lopsided seven-beat metre. In between, the piano breaks into rattling scale exercises, the joke for a graduating piano student.

**Main ideas**

- The dance: a fast, bright tune built on the [[pentatonic scale]].
- The seven-beat tune: lopsided and swinging, with plucked strings like balalaikas.
- The exercise: scales in parallel, the kind pianists practise from the Hanon exercise book.

### Threads

- **Father and son.** Written for Maxim's graduation and birthday, the concerto is pitched for a young player: clear textures, octaves, scales.
- **Exercises into music.** Octaves in the first movement, rocking threes in the second and Hanon scales in the third: the stuff of piano practice turned into tunes.
- **Lightness.** No trumpets, no trombones and few clouds: a rare holiday in Shostakovich's music.
