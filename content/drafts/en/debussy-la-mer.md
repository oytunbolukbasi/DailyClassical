### The big picture

- Debussy wrote *La mer* between 1903 and 1905. He began it far from the coast, in Burgundy, and said he preferred the seas of paintings and books to the real one. He corrected the proofs in the summer of 1905 at a hotel in Eastbourne, on the English Channel.
- He called it "three symphonic sketches" and avoided the word symphony. There are few tunes in the usual sense: a short [[motif]] grows, changes colour and turns into a new one, the way one wave turns into the next.
- The first performance, in Paris on 15 October 1905, was a disappointment. One critic wrote that he could not hear, see or smell the sea. It became a success in 1908, when Debussy conducted it himself.
- Debussy owned a print of Hokusai's *Great Wave* and asked for it to be used on the cover of the first edition.
- In one line: the sea waking from dawn to noon (I), waves at play (II), a storm between wind and water (III).

### I. De l'aube à midi sur la mer

**Summary.** The sea before sunrise, still and dark, slowly fills with light and movement. The music never repeats itself for long; it swells, breaks and re-forms. It ends at noon, in a blaze of brass.

**Main ideas**

- The call: a short, rising figure for cor anglais and [[muted]] trumpet together, heard near the start. It will come back in the last movement.
- The noon [[chorale]]: a broad, slow melody for the brass at the very end.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | A low, soft rumble from the timpani and double basses; harps pluck; muted violins shimmer above | Darkness before dawn. One low note is held underneath, a [[pedal note]] |
| ≈ 0:50 | A short call from the cor anglais and muted trumpet playing together | The first [[motif]]. Remember it |
| ≈ 2:00 | The pace picks up: woodwinds rock back and forth, horns answer, harps ripple | The sea wakes. The main part of the movement begins |
| ≈ 4:45–5:30 | A warm, swaying melody low in the strings, rich and dark | The cellos, split into four groups, play in [[divisi]]. A famous page of the score |
| ≈ 6:30 | Waves of sound build up, each one a little higher | The light grows towards noon |
| Last 90 seconds | The brass play a slow, solemn melody; cymbals and a gong crown it with a final crash | The noon chorale. The sun is at its height |

**Things to notice**

1. Listen to the very end. The brass chorale returns at the end of the third movement, louder and faster.

### II. Jeux de vagues

**Summary.** The lightest movement, close to a [[scherzo]]. Spray, glitter and sudden gusts. Nothing stays in one place for more than a few seconds.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | Trembling strings, a harp [[glissando]], a tap on the triangle, a flicker from the cor anglais | The waves begin to play |
| ≈ 0:45 | Little phrases tossed between oboe, flute and cor anglais; the glockenspiel sparkles | Light on the water |
| ≈ 2:00 | Trills in the woodwinds and violins; the music becomes busier | The game speeds up |
| ≈ 3:30–4:30 | The whole orchestra swells into larger waves, then breaks apart | The movement's climax |
| Last minute | The sound thins out: a harp, the glockenspiel, a few soft notes from the flute | The waves die away. The movement ends almost in silence |

**Things to notice**

1. Count how many instruments get a solo moment. Debussy gives almost every one a few seconds in the light.

### III. Dialogue du vent et de la mer

**Summary.** A storm. The low strings and drums growl, the wind howls in the woodwinds, and the call from the first movement returns. After a calm middle, the noon chorale comes back in triumph.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | A dark rumble from cellos, basses, timpani and bass drum; sudden gusts in the woodwinds | The wind rises over the sea |
| ≈ 0:40 | The muted trumpet plays the short call from the first movement | The first movement's call returns |
| ≈ 2:30–3:30 | The storm drops. An oboe and a flute sing a long, tender melody over high violins | A calm in the middle of the storm |
| ≈ 4:30 | The rumbling returns and gathers speed; strings and brass trade blows | Wind and sea answer each other |
| ≈ 6:30 | The brass ring out the slow noon chorale from the first movement, now loud and fast | The sea in full sunlight |
| Last 30 seconds | A headlong rush; timpani and brass hammer out the final chords | The end |

### Threads

- **The call and the chorale.** Two ideas from the first movement return in the last, so the three sketches become one picture.
- **Colour as shape.** Debussy builds each movement from changes of sound: which instrument plays, how high, how loud. That is where to listen.
- **A Japanese wave.** Like Hokusai's print, the music is precise in its details and huge in its effect.
