### The big picture

- In 1890 Brahms, aged 57, thought he had written enough and could retire. In March 1891 he heard Richard Mühlfeld, the clarinettist of the court orchestra at Meiningen, and that summer at Bad Ischl in the Austrian Alps he wrote two works for him: a Clarinet Trio and this Quintet.
- Mühlfeld first played it in private at Meiningen on 24 November 1891, with the Joachim Quartet. The public premiere followed in Berlin on 12 December.
- The line-up is Mozart's: one clarinet and a string quartet. Mühlfeld had played Mozart's Clarinet Quintet for Brahms, and Brahms ends his own with a set of [[variation]]s, as Mozart did.
- The quintet ends where it began: the last pages of the finale bring back the opening of the first movement.
- In one line: a restless, autumnal Allegro (I), a slow song with a wild, gypsy-style middle (II), a gentle tune that turns into a quick dance (III), variations that lead back to the start (IV).

### I. Allegro

**Summary.** A movement in [[sonata form]] that seems unsure of its key: the opening hovers between B minor and D major, and it takes several bars after the clarinet's entry for B minor to settle. The mood swings between tenderness and outbursts, and the end is quiet.

**Main ideas**

- The opening: two violins in a wavering, rocking figure, a few soft bars that will return at the very end of the work.
- Theme 1: the clarinet's soft, rising arpeggio, followed by a broad, swinging tune in viola and cello.
- Theme 2: a warmer tune in D major, sung by the clarinet over an off-beat murmur in the strings.

### II. Adagio

**Summary.** A slow movement in three parts. The outer parts are a quiet song for clarinet over [[muted]] strings. The middle part bursts into a free, florid lament in the Hungarian "gypsy" style Brahms loved.

**Main ideas**

- The main melody: a slow, rocking song for the clarinet, answered and shadowed by the first violin.
- The middle section: wailing clarinet runs over trembling strings, like an improvisation.

### Threads

- **Beginning and end.** The violins' opening figure frames the whole work: the first bars, the end of the first movement and the last pages of the finale.
- **Five equal voices.** The clarinet rarely stands apart. It shares tunes with the first violin and the viola, and often plays below the strings.
- **Autumn.** Most of the quintet is soft, in the minor, and ends quietly. Even the major-key moments are tinged with regret.
