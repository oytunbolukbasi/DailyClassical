### The big picture

- Composed mainly in the summers of 1895 and 1896 at Steinbach, on the shore of Lake Attersee in Upper Austria, where Mahler had a small composing hut. He conducted the first complete performance in Krefeld on 9 June 1902.
- The longest symphony in the standard repertoire. The first movement alone lasts over half an hour.
- Mahler gave each movement a private title, then withdrew them before publication: "Pan awakes, summer marches in", "What the flowers in the meadow tell me", "What the animals in the forest tell me", "What man tells me", "What the angels tell me" and "What love tells me".
- Two singing movements: a mezzo-soprano sings words by Nietzsche (IV), then women's and boys' voices sing a folk poem about angels (V). The last three movements run on without a break.
- In one line: nature wakes and summer marches in (I), flowers (II), animals (III), the human voice at midnight (IV), angels (V), love (VI).

### I. Kräftig. Entschieden.

**Summary.** Part One of the symphony, and a world in itself. Dark, motionless nature slowly stirs, and summer arrives as a noisy, cheerful march that sweeps everything along.

**Main ideas**

- The horn call: eight horns in [[unison]], a bold striding tune.
- The sleeping earth: low brass, muffled drums, long silences.
- The march of summer: light, chirping woodwinds that grow into a full military band.

1. Mahler wrote "there follows a long pause" at the end of this movement in his manuscript. The symphony's second part starts here.

### VI. Langsam. Ruhevoll. Empfunden.

**Summary.** "What love tells me." A long, slow hymn for orchestra alone, no voices. It begins in near-silence and climbs, with setbacks, to a radiant end in D major.

**Things to notice**

1. The opening melody of this movement is only a few notes long, but it is never quite the same twice. Try to follow it all the way through.

### Threads

- **A ladder of being.** Mahler's plan climbs from rock and flowers to animals, people, angels and love.
- **Distance.** Offstage snare drums in the first movement and the posthorn in the third bring sounds from far away.
- **From D minor to D major.** The symphony opens in D minor and ends in D major, but the long road between them runs through F major and many other keys.
