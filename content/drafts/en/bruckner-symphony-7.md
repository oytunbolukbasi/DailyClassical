### The big picture

- Written between 1881 and 1883 in Vienna and dedicated to King Ludwig II of Bavaria. The premiere, under Arthur Nikisch in Leipzig on 30 December 1884, was the greatest success of Bruckner's life. He was 60.
- Bruckner revered Richard Wagner. He began the slow movement in January 1883, sensing that Wagner, who was ill, would soon die. Wagner died in February, and the movement's ending became a lament for him.
- That movement uses four [[Wagner tuba]]s, instruments made for Wagner's operas. This was their first appearance in a symphony.
- Bruckner was a famous organist, and his orchestra often works like an organ: whole groups of instruments take turns, as if he were changing keyboards.
- In one line: a melody from a dream (I), a hymn of mourning (II), a trumpet-driven dance (III), a brisk, bright finale (IV).

### I. Allegro moderato

**Summary.** A long melody rises out of a shimmer of strings and opens the work. The movement unfolds slowly, in great blocks, and ends in a blaze of E major.

**Main ideas**

- Theme 1: a long, arching melody in the cellos, rising over two octaves. Bruckner said he heard it in a dream, played on a viola.
- Theme 2: a wistful tune in oboe and clarinet that keeps changing key.
- Theme 3: a striding, rhythmic idea, often played in octaves.

**Summary.** "With motion, but not fast." A compact, light-footed finale built from three ideas; the conductor Georg Tintner compared it to a Haydn finale. At the end the symphony's opening melody returns in triumph.

### Threads

- **The dream melody.** The opening cello theme returns at the end of the finale, now in the brass.
- **Wagner.** The Wagner tubas, and the funeral music in the Adagio, are Bruckner's tribute to the composer he revered.
- **Blocks of sound.** Silences and sudden changes of group, as on an organ, divide the music into great slabs.
