### The big picture

- Brahms himself said the symphony took 21 years, from 1855 to 1876. He was haunted by Beethoven and destroyed or reworked many early attempts. Brahms's friend Felix Otto Dessoff conducted the premiere in Karlsruhe on 4 November 1876. Brahms was 43.
- The conductor Hans von Bülow called it "Beethoven's Tenth". The big tune of the finale reminded many of the "Ode to Joy"; Brahms is said to have replied: "any ass can see that".
- The horn call that lights up the finale goes back to September 1868, when Brahms sent it on a card to Clara Schumann: "Thus blew the shepherd's horn today!"
- Trombones play only in the finale, and first enter quietly with that horn call.
- In one line: struggle (I), tenderness (II), a gentle interlude (III), darkness into light (IV).

### I. Un poco sostenuto – Allegro

**Summary.** A slow introduction of enormous tension, then a fierce, driving [[sonata form]] movement. After all the struggle it ends, surprisingly, in a quiet C major.

**Main ideas**

- The introduction: violins climb while the woodwinds fall, over a timpani pounding a single note.
- Theme 1: a restless, strenuous melody in the violins, full of wide leaps.
- Theme 2: a calm, warm tune in the oboe, clarinet and horn.

**Things to notice**

1. The finale's main tune is not the horn call but the striding violin melody at ≈ 4:10. Brahms gives it to the strings quietly first; only later does the whole orchestra take it up.

### Threads

- **From C minor to C major.** The symphony moves from struggle to triumph, a path Beethoven's Fifth had made famous.
- **The shadow of Beethoven.** The fate rhythm in the first movement and the "Ode to Joy" likeness in the finale are both nods to the composer Brahms felt behind him.
- **The horn call.** An alphorn melody, sent to Clara Schumann in 1868, becomes the turning point of the whole work.
