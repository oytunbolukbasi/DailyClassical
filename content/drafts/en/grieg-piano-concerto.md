### The big picture

- Grieg wrote the concerto in the summer of 1868 in Søllerød, near Copenhagen, where he had gone for the country air. He was 24. It is the only concerto he completed.
- He knew Schumann's Piano Concerto well, having heard Clara Schumann play it in Leipzig as a student. He chose the same key, A minor, and a similar opening flourish.
- The first performance was in Copenhagen on 3 April 1869, with Edmund Neupert as soloist. In 1870 Liszt sight-read it from the manuscript in Rome and praised it. Grieg kept revising the concerto for the rest of his life, until a few weeks before his death.
- It sounds Norwegian from the first bar: the falling opening figure is typical of Norwegian folk song, and the finale is a halling, a leaping folk dance, with echoes of the Hardanger fiddle. In 1909 it became the first piano concerto ever recorded, in a version cut to six minutes.
- In one line: a dramatic crash and a song (I), a hushed nocturne for muted strings (II), a stamping folk dance that ends in a hymn (III).

### I. Allegro molto moderato

**Summary.** One of the most famous openings in music: a drum roll, then the piano crashes down the whole keyboard. What follows is gentler than that start suggests, a movement of songs more than battles, in [[sonata form]]. Near the end the piano has a long [[cadenza]] that grows from a whisper to a roar.

**Main ideas**

- The flourish: a falling figure that crashes down the keyboard in loud chords and octaves.
- Theme 1: a quiet, slightly march-like tune, first in the woodwinds, then in the piano.
- Theme 2: a warm, singing melody in C major, first on the cellos.

2. Liszt suggested giving Theme 2 to a trumpet instead of the cellos. Grieg tried it, then changed it back.

### II. Adagio

**Summary.** A slow, hushed song in D-flat major, far from the A minor of the first movement. The [[muted]] strings play a long, calm melody; the piano answers with its own decorated, dreamy music, and later takes the melody itself, in full, ringing chords. The end leads straight into the finale.

**Main ideas**

- The string melody: long, slow and quiet, on muted violins, rising and falling gently.
- The piano's song: delicate, decorated phrases, with quick little groups of five and six notes.

### III. Allegro moderato molto e marcato – Quasi presto – Andante maestoso

**Summary.** A Norwegian dance finale. The piano stamps out a halling, a leaping folk dance, against sharp chords in the orchestra. In the middle a solo flute plays a calm, high melody, like a hymn to nature. At the end that hymn returns in full glory, and the concerto finishes in A major.

**Main ideas**

- The dance: an accented tune in 2/4, with piano and orchestra trading the strong and weak beats. It is modelled on the halling.
- The hymn: a calm, wide melody in F major, first on a solo flute in its highest register.

### Threads

- **Norway in the concert hall.** Folk-song shapes in the first movement, a halling in the last. Grieg was 24 and wanted his music to sound like home.
- **Song before battle.** The piano spends as much time singing and accompanying as it does showing off.
- **From A minor to A major.** The concerto starts with a dark crash and ends with a hymn in the major.
