### The big picture

- Beethoven sketched the trio in 1810 and finished it in March 1811. He dedicated it to Archduke Rudolph of Austria, the Emperor's youngest brother, who was his piano and composition pupil, his friend and one of his most faithful patrons. Beethoven dedicated more than a dozen works to him, among them the *Emperor* Concerto and the *Missa solemnis*. The nickname comes from that dedication.
- The first public performance took place on 11 April 1814 at the Viennese hotel Zum römischen Kaiser, with Beethoven at the piano, Ignaz Schuppanzigh (violin) and Joseph Linke (cello). By then he was very deaf. The violinist Louis Spohr, at a rehearsal, wrote that in loud passages he pounded until the strings jangled, and in soft ones whole groups of notes went missing. After a repeat a few weeks later, Beethoven never played in public as a pianist again.
- It is long and spacious: more than forty minutes, with the piano a full partner and often the leader rather than an accompanist. The scherzo comes second and the slow movement third, the reverse of the usual order, and the slow movement runs straight into the finale without a break.
- In one line: a broad, singing opening (I), a sly scherzo with a creeping trio (II), a hymn and variations (III), a lively, rustic rondo (IV).

### I. Allegro moderato

**Summary.** The piano alone sings a broad, noble tune; the violin and cello take it up. The movement is a [[sonata form]] that seems in no hurry. Its most magical passage comes at the end of the [[development]], when the strings pluck and the piano trills, almost in a whisper.

**Main ideas**

- Theme 1: a calm, warm melody in the piano, moving in even steps, like a slow march for strings.
- Theme 2: a lighter, rising tune in an unexpected, brighter key, G major.

### II. Scherzo. Allegro

**Summary.** A light, mischievous [[scherzo]] that begins with the cello alone, climbing a scale. The middle section, the [[trio]], is strange and shadowy: a slow, chromatic, creeping tune that the instruments take up one after another, broken by a loud, swinging waltz. Beethoven writes out the plan in full: scherzo, trio, scherzo, trio, scherzo, then a short [[coda]].

**Main ideas**

- The scherzo: a rising scale in the cello, answered by the violin, with a skipping, dance-like tune.
- The trio, part 1: a winding, chromatic line in a low register, in B-flat minor, built as a [[fugato]].
- The trio, part 2: a loud, cheerful waltz in the piano.

**Listening stops**

**Things to notice**

1. Listen for the cello at the start. Beginning a scherzo with a single, quiet low instrument playing a scale is a joke on the listener: it sounds like a warm-up.
2. In the trio, follow the creeping line from instrument to instrument. It moves by the smallest steps on the keyboard, which is what makes it sound uneasy.

**In this recording.** The scherzo lasts twelve minutes here because Kempff, Szeryng and Fournier play every repeat Beethoven wrote, including the second trio. Some recordings are much shorter: Ashkenazy, Perlman and Harrell take about six and a half minutes.

### III. Andante cantabile ma però con moto

**Summary.** A slow, hymn-like theme in D major, followed by four [[variation]]s in which the notes move faster each time, while the harmony stays the same. Then the music slows, the theme seems to lose its way, and a long, hesitant [[coda]] leads without a break into the finale.

**Main ideas**

- The theme: a calm, prayer-like melody in chords, first in the piano, then in the strings.
- The variations: the same harmony with ever quicker notes: triplets, then flowing semiquavers, then a shimmering rush, then a return to the theme's calm.

**Things to notice**

1. Hum the theme's first notes and keep them in mind. Each variation runs faster on top, but underneath the same slow chords pass by at the same pace.
2. At the end, notice that there is no gap: the finale begins on the edge of the slow music's last chord.

### IV. Finale. Allegro moderato – Presto

**Summary.** After the hush, a jaunty, slightly lopsided tune bursts in. The finale is a [[rondo]]: the tune keeps returning between other episodes. At the end the tempo jumps to a fast *Presto* in 6/8 that strays into far-off keys before racing home.

**Main ideas**

- The rondo tune: a cheerful, bouncing melody in the piano, with accents that fall slightly off the beat.
- The *Presto*: a quick, skipping version of the tune in groups of three.

### Threads

- **Three equals.** The piano leads but does not dominate. The cello sings high and alone, the violin carries tunes, and the famous passages, the pizzicato in the first movement and the creeping trio, need all three.
- **Calm.** Two of the four movements are slow or broad, and the slow movement sits third. The weight of the trio lies in its quiet music.
- **Beethoven's farewell as a pianist.** He gave the first public performance himself in 1814, already very deaf. It was among his last appearances at the keyboard.
