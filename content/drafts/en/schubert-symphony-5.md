### The big picture

- Schubert wrote the symphony in September 1816 and dated the score as finished on 3 October 1816. He was nineteen. It was meant for an amateur orchestra in Vienna founded by Otto Hatwig, in which Schubert played the viola and his brother Ferdinand the violin, and that orchestra gave its first performance that autumn.
- That June he wrote in his diary: "O Mozart! immortal Mozart!" The symphony uses the smallest orchestra of all his symphonies: one flute, two oboes, two bassoons, two horns and strings, with no clarinets, trumpets or timpani. Many have compared its light, clear scoring to Mozart's.
- It was not played in public until 17 October 1841, thirteen years after Schubert's death, and not printed until the 1880s, in the first complete edition of his works, edited by Brahms.
- It does not sound like an imitation. Schubert slides into distant keys, turns from major to minor in a moment, and starts the return of the first movement in the "wrong" key.
- In one line: a bright, quick opening (I), a song without words (II), a stormy minuet with a country-dance trio (III), a light-footed dance to finish (IV).

### I. Allegro

**Summary.** No slow introduction: four bars of woodwind chords and a quick run in the violins, and the main tune is already there. The movement is a [[sonata form]] full of sunlight, with a gentle second theme and a short, wandering development.

**Main ideas**

- The curtain: four bars of soft woodwind chords, with a rising run in the first violins.
- Theme 1: a cheerful, rising tune in a dotted rhythm, soft, in the violins, echoed by the basses.
- Theme 2: a graceful, singing tune in F major, first in the strings, then in the woodwind.

### Threads

- **A small orchestra.** One flute, pairs of oboes, bassoons and horns, and strings. With no trumpets or drums, every instrument can be heard, and the loud moments stay light.
- **Mozart and beyond.** The clarity and balance recall Mozart, whom Schubert adored that year, but the sudden slides into distant keys are Schubert's own.
- **Music among friends.** It was written for an orchestra of amateurs and family, in which the composer himself played viola.
