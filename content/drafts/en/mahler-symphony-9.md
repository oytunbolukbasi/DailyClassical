### The big picture

- Mahler worked on the Ninth in the summer of 1909 at Toblach, in the Tyrolean mountains, where he had his last composing hut. It is the last symphony he completed.
- He never heard it. Bruno Walter conducted the first performance in Vienna on 26 June 1912, a year after Mahler's death.
- It is often heard as a farewell: Mahler had lost his young daughter in 1907 and had been told he had a heart condition. Yet while writing it he was busy and planning ahead, so the farewell may be the listener's as much as the composer's.
- An unusual shape: the first and last movements are slow, the middle two fast. It begins in D major and ends a semitone lower, in D-flat major, as if the ground had sunk.
- In one line: a farewell to the world (I), clumsy country dances (II), defiance and fury (III), a hymn that fades into silence (IV).

### I. Andante comodo

**Summary.** A slow movement that keeps breaking into violence. A gentle, sighing melody tries again and again to sing, and each time it is overwhelmed. Alban Berg called it the most glorious movement Mahler wrote, full of love for the earth.

**Main ideas**

- The limp: an uneven, hesitating rhythm in the cellos and horn.
- The sigh: a falling two-note phrase in the second violins, the main theme.
- The bell: four notes tolled on the harp.

**Things to notice**

1. Do not reach for the next track when the music seems to stop. The final minutes are mostly silence, and the silence is part of the piece.

**In this recording.** Barbirolli was famously thorough with Mahler: his first performance of the Ninth took nearly fifty hours of rehearsal. He worked regularly with the Berlin Philharmonic from 1961, and made this recording with them in January 1964.

### Threads

- **The sinking key.** D major at the start, D-flat major at the end: the symphony ends a semitone lower than it began.
- **The turn.** A small ornamental figure, sung by the trumpet in the Rondo-Burleske, becomes the heart of the Adagio.
- **Breaking off.** In every movement the music builds and then falls apart, until in the last pages it simply stops.
