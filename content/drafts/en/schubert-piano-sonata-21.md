### The big picture

- Schubert wrote his last three piano sonatas in September 1828 and finished this one on the 26th. Two days later he played from them at an evening gathering in Vienna. He died on 19 November, aged 31.
- No publisher took them in his lifetime. Anton Diabelli published them about ten years later and dedicated them to Robert Schumann, because Johann Nepomuk Hummel, the pianist Schubert had chosen, had died.
- The first movement is long and unhurried, marked *Molto moderato*. Its calm opening melody is broken by a soft, low [[trill]] deep in the bass, a sound that comes back at the movement's key moments.
- Schubert writes for the piano as for a singer: long melodies in the middle of the keyboard, with the [[sustain pedal]] letting the harmonies ring around them.
- In one line: a calm song haunted by a trill (I), a lament over a rocking rhythm (II), a light, delicate scherzo (III), a finale that seems to start in the wrong key and finds its way home (IV).

### I. Molto moderato

**Summary.** A long movement in [[sonata form]] built on a quiet, hymn-like melody. Its [[exposition]] moves through three keys instead of the usual two, and a low trill keeps opening doors into distant ones.

**Main ideas**

- Theme 1: a calm, singing melody in the middle of the keyboard over soft, even chords.
- The trill: a soft rumble on a low G-flat, far below the melody, followed by silence.
- Theme 2: a darker melody in F-sharp minor over a pulsing accompaniment in threes.

### II. Andante sostenuto

**Summary.** A slow movement in C-sharp minor, a key very far from the sonata's home. A grave melody moves over a rocking rhythm; a hymn-like middle section brings warmth, and one sudden change of key near the end is like a door opening.

**Main ideas**

- The main melody: slow and grave, in the middle of the keyboard, over a rocking figure in which the left hand reaches over the right to touch high notes.
- The middle section: a hymn-like melody in A major over a flowing, rippling accompaniment.

### IV. Allegro ma non troppo – Presto

**Summary.** A [[rondo]]-like finale. Its theme begins on a bare, held octave and seems to start in the wrong key, then finds its way to B-flat. It ends with a fast, loud dash.

**Main ideas**

- The main theme: a single held octave on G, then a skipping tune that sounds like C minor before it settles in B-flat major.
- Theme 2: a long, flowing melody over an unbroken stream of quick notes.
- Theme 3: a loud, stamping theme in F minor with a dotted rhythm.

### Threads

- **The trill.** The first movement's low trill on G-flat leads to every distant key in the sonata; the finale's bare G seems to answer it.
- **Far-away keys.** G-flat, F-sharp minor, C-sharp minor: the sonata keeps wandering from B-flat, like the wanderer of the song it quotes.
- **Silence.** Pauses after the trills, fragments in the finale's coda: Schubert lets the music stop and listen.
