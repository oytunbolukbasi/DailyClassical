### The big picture

- *Spring* is the first of the four concertos known as *The Four Seasons*. Vivaldi probably wrote them between about 1718 and 1723, partly while he was music director at the court of Mantua, and published them in Amsterdam in 1725 as the first four of the twelve concertos in his Op. 8, *Il cimento dell'armonia e dell'inventione* ("The contest between harmony and invention").
- Each concerto came with a sonnet, possibly written by Vivaldi himself. The spring sonnet has three parts, one for each movement: birds, streams and a storm; a goatherd asleep in a meadow; nymphs and shepherds dancing to a bagpipe. Vivaldi printed the lines in the score next to the music that paints them, and even marked the parts ("the barking dog").
- It is a concerto in the Baroque way: a refrain for the whole string band, the [[ritornello]], keeps coming back, and between its returns the solo violin paints the scenes of the poem. A harpsichord and bass instruments, the [[continuo]], hold everything together from underneath.
- Instrumental music that tells a story was rare in 1725. *The Four Seasons* is one of the earliest and most detailed examples of what was later called [[programme music]].
- In one line: birds, a stream and a storm (I), a goatherd asleep in the heat (II), a country dance to the bagpipe (III).

### I. Allegro

**Summary.** "Spring has arrived." A bright, dancing refrain for all the strings opens the movement and returns between the scenes of the sonnet: birdsong, murmuring streams, a short thunderstorm, and the birds again once the storm has passed.

**Main ideas**

- The refrain: a cheerful tune for all the strings, each phrase played loud and then echoed softly.
- The birds: the solo violin and two other violins, very high, with trills and chirping repeated notes.
- The storm: fast repeated notes in the whole band (thunder) and quick rushing runs from the soloist (lightning).

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | The whole string band plays a bright, skipping tune; each short phrase is repeated quietly, like an echo | The [[ritornello]]: "Spring has arrived" |
| ≈ 0:30 | The band falls away; the solo violin and two other violins chirp and trill high up, answering each other | The birds greet the spring with song |
| ≈ 1:05 | The refrain returns briefly | The ritornello again |
| ≈ 1:13 | The violins play soft, flowing runs of short notes, close together, like water | "The streams flow with a sweet murmur" |
| ≈ 1:44 | The whole band hammers fast repeated notes; the soloist answers with rapid runs shooting upwards | Thunder and lightning. The music turns towards a minor key |
| ≈ 2:13 | The refrain, now darker, in a minor key | The storm is passing |
| ≈ 2:20 | The high trills come back in the solo violin | "The birds return to their song" |
| ≈ 3:02 | The refrain one last time, ending softly | The closing ritornello |

**Things to notice**

1. Listen for the echo in the refrain: loud, then soft, then loud again. It is the same idea as the call and answer of the birds.
2. In the storm, the thunder is the band and the lightning is the soloist. Try to hear the two layers separately.

### II. Largo e pianissimo sempre

**Summary.** A short, still scene in C-sharp minor: a goatherd asleep in a flowery meadow, his dog beside him. Three layers sound at once, each with a meaning: the violins are the rustling leaves, the solo violin is the sleeper's long, calm song, and the violas are the dog.

**Main ideas**

- The leaves: the violins play a soft, rocking pattern of two short notes, over and over.
- The sleeper: a long, slow melody in the solo violin, floating above the leaves.
- The dog: the violas repeat one loud, rough note on every beat. Vivaldi labelled the part "the barking dog" and asked for it loud.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | Very quiet rocking notes in the violins; underneath, one note repeated firmly on every beat | The leaves rustle; the viola "dog" starts barking. Vivaldi wrote no bass part for this movement |
| ≈ 0:05 | The solo violin enters with a slow, long-breathed melody | The goatherd sleeps |
| ≈ 1:20 | The melody climbs higher and moves to a brighter key for a moment | The middle of the dream |
| Near the end | The solo line sinks back to where it began; the dog keeps barking to the last bar | The scene stays exactly as it was |

**Things to notice**

1. The bass is missing. Vivaldi left the cellos and basses out of this movement, so the music seems to float, which suits a scene of sleep.
2. The "dog" is loud while everything else is marked very soft. Vivaldi wanted the contrast.

### III. Danza pastorale. Allegro

**Summary.** A country dance in a lilting rhythm of three-note groups. Under the tune, the lower strings hold long notes like the [[drone]] of a bagpipe, and the solo violin leads the nymphs and shepherds through several rounds of the dance.

**Main ideas**

- The dance: a swinging tune in the violins, in a long-short rhythm.
- The bagpipe: low held notes in the violas and bass, which barely move.
- The solo violin's turns: brighter, more decorated passages between the returns of the dance.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | A swinging dance tune over low, held notes that hardly change | The ritornello, over a bagpipe [[drone]] |
| ≈ 0:30 | The solo violin steps forward with its own, more decorated line | The first solo episode: the dancers start |
| ≈ 0:55 | The dance tune again, the whole band | The ritornello returns |
| ≈ 1:10 | The solo violin again, with wider leaps and quicker notes | A second episode |
| ≈ 1:50 | The dance turns darker: the same rhythm in a minor key | A minor-key round of the dance, a brief cloud |
| ≈ 2:30 | The soloist runs up and down in fast notes; the drone continues below | The last and most brilliant episode |
| ≈ 3:05 | The dance tune, back in the bright home key | The final ritornello |

**Things to notice**

1. Listen to the bass line rather than the tune. For long stretches it stays on the same notes, the way a bagpipe's drone pipes do.

**In this recording.** Simon Standage and The English Concert play on period instruments, built or set up as in Vivaldi's time. Trevor Pinnock directs from the harpsichord, which plays chords under the band as part of the [[continuo]].

### Threads

- **The sonnet.** Every scene in the music follows a line of the poem, in order: the birds, the streams, the storm, the birds again, the sleeper and his dog, the dance.
- **Refrain and episodes.** In both fast movements a returning refrain frames the scenes. The slow movement has no refrain at all, only one still picture.
- **Who plays what.** Vivaldi turns the parts of the string band into characters: two violins become birds, the violas become a dog, the basses become a bagpipe.
