### The big picture

- Chopin wrote this concerto in Warsaw between the autumn of 1829 and the early spring of 1830, when he had just finished his studies. He was nineteen. It was the first of his two piano concertos; it is called No. 2 only because it was published second.
- After two run-throughs at home and among friends, he played it in public on 17 March 1830 at the National Theatre in Warsaw. It was his first big concert in his own city. Seven months later he left Poland, and he never returned.
- In a letter of 3 October 1829 to his friend Tytus Woyciechowski he wrote of his "ideal", whom he had loved in silence for six months, and who "inspired the Adagio of my Concerto". His biographers agree that she was the young singer Konstancja Gładkowska. The published concerto, however, is dedicated to Countess Delfina Potocka.
- As in his other concerto, the orchestra frames the piano rather than competing with it. The piano sings like an opera singer, in long, ornamented melodies, and the dances of Poland are never far away: a mazurka rhythm in the first movement, a kujawiak in the last.
- In one line: a proud, sorrowful Allegro in a mazurka's rhythm (I), a nocturne for a secret love (II), a Polish country dance that ends in F major (III).

### I. Maestoso

**Summary.** The orchestra first presents the two main themes on its own: a proud, sorrowful tune in a dotted rhythm, and a gentle song in a major key. Then the piano enters with a dramatic flourish and makes both themes its own, decorated with runs and trills. The movement is in [[sonata form]].

**Main ideas**

- Theme 1: a solemn, dotted tune in F minor, with the lilt of a slow mazurka.
- Theme 2: a tender, singing melody in A-flat major, like a nocturne.

1. Listen to how long the orchestra plays before the piano enters, over three minutes here. When the piano comes in, notice how it takes the same tunes and turns them into something more personal and decorated.
2. Follow the dotted rhythm of the first theme. It keeps returning, in the orchestra and the piano, through the whole movement.

**In this recording.** Zimerman recorded both concertos in 1999 with the Polish Festival Orchestra, which he formed for the 150th anniversary of Chopin's death and directed from the piano. His tempos are unusually broad: the slow movement lasts over eleven minutes, where many pianists take about nine. On the Spotify album the concerto is tracks 4–6, after the First Concerto.

### II. Larghetto

**Summary.** A [[nocturne]] for piano and orchestra, and the movement Chopin wrote for his "ideal". The piano sings a long, slow melody, and each time it returns it is more richly decorated, like an opera singer ornamenting an aria. In the middle the mood breaks: over trembling strings, the piano declaims a dramatic, speech-like passage in octaves.

**Main ideas**

- The song: a long, slow, singing melody in A-flat major, decorated with trills and runs.
- The outburst: a passionate passage in the minor, the piano in bare octaves over a string [[tremolo]], like an operatic [[recitative]].

### III. Allegro vivace

**Summary.** A finale in the rhythm of the [[kujawiak]], a Polish country dance in three. Its main tune keeps coming back like a refrain, as in a [[rondo]]. In one episode the string players strike the strings with the wood of their bows, and near the end a horn call turns the music from F minor to a bright F major.

**Main ideas**

- The refrain: a graceful, lilting dance tune in F minor, first in the piano.
- The rustic episode: a robust village tune in the piano, over string players tapping with the wood of the bow.
- The horn call: a short signal that opens the F major ending.

1. In the second episode, listen for the clicking sound under the piano tune. It is the string players tapping with the wood of their bows, an unusual effect at the time.
2. Listen for the moment the horn sounds its call. Within seconds, the minor key of the whole concerto gives way to F major.

### Threads

- **The singer.** Chopin heard melody as a singer would, and his piano phrases breathe like long, ornamented arias, nowhere more than in the Larghetto.
- **Poland.** The mazurka rhythm of the first movement and the kujawiak of the finale bring the dances of his homeland into the concert hall.
- **Minor to major.** The concerto stays in the minor until its last minute and a half, and then turns to F major.
