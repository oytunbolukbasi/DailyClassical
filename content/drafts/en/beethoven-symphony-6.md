### The big picture

- Beethoven loved the countryside and spent much of his time walking in it; he often left Vienna to work in the country. His first sketches for this symphony date from 1802, and he finished it in 1808, alongside the Fifth.
- Both symphonies were first heard in the same concert, on 22 December 1808 at the Theater an der Wien: a long, under-rehearsed programme of about four hours.
- Each movement has a title by Beethoven, from "Awakening of cheerful feelings on arriving in the country" to "Shepherd's song". But he insisted in the programme of the first performance that the symphony was "more the expression of feeling than painting".
- It has five movements instead of the usual four, and the last three run together without a pause: a village dance is cut short by a storm, and the storm clears into a song of thanks.
- In one line: arriving in the country (I), a brook with birdsong (II), a village dance (III), a thunderstorm (IV), a shepherds' hymn of thanks (V).

### I. Awakening of cheerful feelings on arriving in the country

**Summary.** No drama: a low, open drone and a short, folk-like tune, like stepping out into fresh air. Beethoven builds whole passages by repeating one small figure over and over, the way a field or a hedgerow repeats a pattern. It is a [[sonata form]], but its mood barely changes.

**Main ideas**

- The drone: a low, open chord held by violas and cellos, like a bagpipe.
- Theme 1: a short tune in the violins; its little second bar is the figure Beethoven repeats again and again.
- Theme 2: a gentle falling melody over a rocking accompaniment in the cellos.

### II. Scene by the brook

**Summary.** A slow, murmuring movement. The strings make the sound of running water while the violins sing a long, lazy melody above it. Near the end the music stops for three birds, which Beethoven names in the score.

**Main ideas**

- The brook: a soft, rocking figure in the lower strings, led by two [[muted]] solo cellos.
- The melody: a slow, gentle tune in the first violins, floating over the water.
- The birds: nightingale (flute), quail (oboe) and cuckoo (two clarinets).

**Listening stops**

**Things to notice**

1. Listen under the melody. The murmuring water runs through almost the entire movement.
2. In the birdsong, try to tell the three birds apart: one trills, one repeats a quick three-note call, one sings two falling notes.

### III. Merry gathering of country people

**Summary.** A village dance. A light tune in the strings is answered by the whole orchestra; then a little band strikes up, with an oboe a fraction off the beat and a bassoon that seems to know only three notes. A heavy, stamping dance follows. Everything is played twice, and then the dance speeds up until it is cut off by the storm.

**Main ideas**

- The dance: a quick tune in three, soft in the strings and loud in reply.
- The village band: a jaunty oboe tune over a bassoon playing just a few notes.
- The stamping dance: a rough, heavy dance in two.

**Listening stops**

### IV. Thunderstorm

**Summary.** The shortest movement and the loudest. A distant rumble, the first drops of rain, and then the storm breaks with full force. The timpani, silent until now, are the thunder; a piccolo shrieks like lightning. Then the storm moves away, and the music opens straight into the finale.

**Main ideas**

- The rumble: a low, quiet tremble in cellos and basses.
- The storm: rushing strings, timpani thunder, piccolo lightning, and trombones at the height.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |

| ≈ 0:20 | The full orchestra crashes in; the timpani thunder; the strings rush up and down | The storm breaks. The timpani play for the first time in the symphony |
| ≈ 1:10 | A high, piercing piccolo above the orchestra | Lightning. The piccolo plays only in this movement |

**Things to notice**

### V. Shepherd's song: cheerful and thankful feelings after the storm

**Summary.** After the storm, a shepherd's call on the clarinet and the horn, and then a broad, swaying hymn of thanks in the violins. The tune returns again and again, each time more richly dressed, rises to a glowing climax and ends in a hushed, prayer-like [[coda]].

**Main ideas**

- The shepherd's call: a rocking, yodel-like phrase, first in the clarinet, then in the horn, over a drone.
- The hymn: a broad, swaying tune in six beats, the shepherds' song of thanks.

### Threads

- **Feeling, not painting.** The brook, the birds and the storm are there to hear, but Beethoven's own note asks us to listen for the mood they leave behind.
- **Drones.** Long held notes run through the symphony: the open chord of the first bars, the village bassoon, the drone under the shepherd's call. They give it the sound of country music.
- **One continuous ending.** The dance, the storm and the hymn run together without a break, a single story from fun to fear to thanks.
- **Held-back instruments.** The timpani and piccolo play only in the storm; the trombones enter in the storm and stay for the hymn.
