### The big picture

- Beethoven wrote the symphony mainly in 1803–04. It was first played privately in the Vienna palace of his patron, Prince Lobkowitz, in 1804, and first heard in public on 7 April 1805 at the Theater an der Wien.
- He meant to name it after Napoleon Bonaparte, then First Consul of France. The story told by his pupil Ferdinand Ries goes that when Napoleon proclaimed himself emperor in May 1804, Beethoven tore the title page in half. A surviving copy of the score still shows "Bonaparte" scratched out. It was published in 1806 as *Sinfonia eroica*, "composed to celebrate the memory of a great man", and dedicated to Lobkowitz.
- Nothing this long had been written as a symphony before: the first movement alone is almost as long as a whole symphony by Haydn. Early listeners were divided, and many found it too long and too strange.
- The finale is built on a tune Beethoven had already used in his ballet *The Creatures of Prometheus* (1801), about the hero who brought fire to humankind.
- In one line: a heroic struggle (I), a funeral march (II), life rushing back with three horns (III), a celebration built from a bare bass line (IV).

### I. Allegro con brio

**Summary.** Two hammer-blow chords, then a plain tune in the cellos. The tune is knocked off course again and again, by a wrong note, by rhythms that pull against the beat and by violent, clashing chords. The movement is the story of how it comes through. It is a [[sonata form]] on a scale nobody had tried before.

**Main ideas**

- Theme 1, in the cellos: a simple tune that outlines the home chord, then slips onto an unexpected note and hangs there.
- The second group: several short ideas rather than one big tune, starting with a gentle falling figure passed from instrument to instrument.
- The new theme: a sad, singing melody in E minor that appears in the middle of the movement, far from home.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | Two loud, short chords from the whole orchestra | The home key, E-flat major, stated in two blows |
| ≈ 0:03 | The cellos play a plain tune; after a few bars it slides onto a strange note, and the violins answer with an uneasy throbbing | Theme 1. The wrong note (C-sharp) is the first crack in the hero's armour |
| ≈ 0:50 | A gentle falling figure is passed from oboe to clarinet to flute to violins | The second group of themes, in B-flat major |
| ≈ 2:30 | Loud, jabbing chords that seem to beat in two against the three of the bar | A [[hemiola]]: the rhythm stumbles on purpose. The climax of the [[exposition]] |
| ≈ 3:00 | The cellos' tune is broken into fragments and pushed into darker keys | The [[development]] begins |
| ≈ 4:35–5:25 | The instruments enter one after another on a busy figure; then a long series of hammered, grinding chords at full volume | A short [[fugato]], then the most violent passage in the movement |
| ≈ 5:30 | Out of the wreckage, the oboes sing a new, sad melody | A new theme, in E minor |

| ≈ 10:50 | The music sets off again, as if for a second development; the E minor melody returns | The [[coda]], one of the longest Beethoven wrote |
| Last minute | The cellos' tune, now in the horns and then the whole orchestra, rises again and again | For the first time it is heard whole, as a fanfare |

**Things to notice**

1. Follow the cellos' tune through the movement. Almost every time it appears it is bent or interrupted, until the coda, where it finally plays out in full.
2. Listen for the single quiet horn just before the recapitulation. It sounds like a mistake. Ries thought so at the first rehearsal, said so, and later wrote that he was close to having his ears boxed.

**In this recording.** Karajan recorded all nine symphonies with the Berlin Philharmonic three times for Deutsche Grammophon; this is the second cycle, made in the Philharmonie in Berlin. He does not repeat the exposition of the first movement, which is why it lasts about thirteen and a half minutes; performances that take the repeat are several minutes longer. On the Spotify album the symphony is tracks 1–4, followed by the *Leonore* Overture No. 3.

### II. Marcia funebre: Adagio assai

**Summary.** A funeral march in C minor, slow and heavy, with a shuddering figure in the basses like muffled drums. A short, consoling middle section in C major gives way to a [[fugue]] and an outburst of grief. At the end the march tune falls apart into fragments separated by silence.

**Main ideas**

- The march: a low, sombre tune in the violins over a growling figure in the basses.
- The second tune: a gentler phrase in the major that soon sinks back into the minor.
- The major section: a brighter melody for oboe, flute and bassoon over flowing strings.

**Listening stops**

**Things to notice**

1. Listen to the basses in the first minute. Their short growl after each phrase sounds like a drum roll in a real funeral procession.
2. In the last bars the tune is broken into pieces with silence between them, as if the mourners can no longer speak.

### III. Scherzo: Allegro vivace

**Summary.** After the funeral, life rushes back. Quiet, fast and nervous at first, the [[scherzo]] gathers until the whole orchestra bursts out. The middle section, the [[trio]], belongs to three horns, one more than symphonies of the time normally used.

**Main ideas**

- The scherzo: quick, whispered short notes in the strings, with a little tune in the oboe.
- The trio: a ringing fanfare for three horns, like a hunting call.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≈ 0:00 | The strings whisper fast, short notes; an oboe picks out a little tune | The scherzo begins very quietly, as if from a distance, and not yet in the home key |
| ≈ 0:50 | Suddenly the whole orchestra bursts in, loud | The tune arrives in the home key, E-flat major |
| ≈ 3:00 | Three horns play a ringing fanfare; strings and woodwinds answer softly | The [[trio]]. The third horn was the novelty |
| ≈ 4:35 | The fast, whispering strings return | The scherzo again |
| ≈ 5:40 | A loud descending figure in [[unison]] suddenly lurches into a different rhythm, in two instead of three | Beethoven changes the metre for four bars |
| Last 15 seconds | A quick build from very soft to very loud, and a sharp ending | The [[coda]] |

**Things to notice**

1. In the trio, listen for the three horns playing together in close harmony. In Beethoven's day these were natural horns without valves, the instrument of the hunt.

### IV. Finale: Allegro molto

**Summary.** A set of [[variation]]s that starts from almost nothing. After a rushing opening, Beethoven presents only the bass line of his theme, plucked and bare. He builds on it layer by layer before the real melody appears, and then takes it through two fugues, a dance, a slow hymn and a final dash.

**Main ideas**

- The bass: a skeleton of a tune, plucked by the strings, with long pauses.
- The melody: a cheerful, simple tune first heard in the oboe; it comes from Beethoven's *Prometheus* ballet.

**Listening stops**

| Time | What you hear | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |

| ≈ 0:45 | The same bass line, now bowed, with new lines woven around it | The first [[variation]]s: the theme is being built up |
| ≈ 1:10 | A cheerful, simple tune appears in the oboe, on top of the bass line | The real melody, from the *Prometheus* ballet |
| ≈ 2:20 | The strings enter one after another on a quiet, busy subject, building tension | A [[fugue]] on the bass line |

| ≈ 4:15 | Fierce and stamping, in the minor | A wild, dance-like variation in G minor |

**Things to notice**

1. The finale begins with only the bass of the theme. Notice how long Beethoven makes you wait for the melody, and how familiar it seems when it arrives.

### Threads

- **A hero's story.** Struggle (I), death (II), life returning (III), celebration (IV). This reading is old, and Beethoven's title, "to celebrate the memory of a great man", invites it.
- **The tune that came first.** The finale's theme existed before the symphony. Some scholars hear its outline already in the cellos' tune of the first movement, so that the whole symphony grows towards it.
- **Horns.** The early horn entry in the first movement, the three horns of the trio and the horns' blaze in the finale: the hunting instrument becomes the voice of the hero.
